\section{Structural Irreducibility in Hadronic Spectroscopy: A
Two-Invariant
Classification}\label{structural-irreducibility-in-hadronic-spectroscopy-a-two-invariant-classification}}

\textbf{Lando Mills}

\emph{Interdependent}

Correspondence: c.landonmills@gmail.com

\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

\textbf{Keywords:} exotic hadrons; tetraquark; pentaquark; topological
winding number; baryon stability; stoichiometric type; di-meson
molecules; Skyrme model; hadronic spectroscopy; invariants

\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

\hypertarget{abstract}{%
\subsection*{Abstract}\label{abstract}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{Abstract}

We propose that the correct basis for classifying hadronic
configurations is not quark content but class membership,
determined by two independent invariants: the topological winding number
of the field configuration and the stoichiometric type of the
colour-singlet decomposition. Configurations sharing both invariants
with a simpler reference object are not new particle species; they are
composites of that reference object --- a conclusion that follows from
the composition principle, which states that class is
exhausted by these two invariants and admits no further refinement from
kinematic data. We show that the tetraquark (qqq̄q̄) shares both
invariants with the meson (qq̄) and is therefore a di-meson molecule, not
an exotic hadron. The pentaquark (qqqqq̄) does not share the meson's
stoichiometric type and is therefore genuinely irreducible --- a new
class. The baryon's topological winding number is
integer-quantized and distinct from all other configurations; baryon
number conservation follows from this quantization without additional
postulate. All three conclusions are derived from invariants that any
particle physicist already accepts. No new dynamical model is proposed.

\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

\hypertarget{introduction}{%
\subsection{Introduction}\label{introduction}}

The proliferation of XYZ states since the observation of X(3872) by the
Belle collaboration {[}1{]} has reopened a question that the quark model
was thought to have settled: what constitutes a new species of hadron?
The quark model answers by quark content. The observation of states with
four or five valence quarks led immediately to the category of
\emph{exotic hadron} --- the tetraquark and the pentaquark.

This categorization by content has not resolved the problem. Two
interpretations --- compact multiquark state and hadronic molecule ---
are dynamically consistent with the data for most observed XYZ states
{[}2{]}. QCD alone does not discriminate between them. The question is
not primarily dynamical; it is structural. What does it mean for a
hadronic configuration to be \emph{genuinely new} as opposed to a
composite of existing configurations?

We answer this question using two invariants. The first is the
topological winding number of the field configuration, which
distinguishes configurations homotopic to the vacuum from those with
non-trivial Skyrmion character. The second is the stoichiometric type of
the colour-singlet decomposition, which classifies the structural
balance of quark and antiquark contributions. We show that these two
invariants together determine class completely: any hadronic
configuration that shares both invariants with a simpler reference
object is a composite of that object, not a new species.

This is a structural principle, not a dynamical one. It does not depend
on the binding mechanism, the constituent model, or the assumed
quark-gluon dynamics. It depends only on what the configuration's field
topology and quark balance actually are --- quantities that can be
assigned without solving QCD.

\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

\hypertarget{two-structural-invariants}{%
\subsection{Two Structural Invariants}\label{two-structural-invariants}}

\hypertarget{topological-winding-number}{%
\subsubsection{Topological Winding
Number}\label{topological-winding-number}}

The Skyrme model {[}3{]} establishes that baryons are topological
solitons: they are not primarily bound states of quarks but
configurations of the meson field that wrap non-trivially around the
three-sphere. The topological charge is the degree of the map
\(U : S^3 \to \mathrm{SU}(2) \cong S^3\) defined by the pion field at
spatial infinity, and it takes integer values:

\[B = \frac{1}{24\pi^2} \int \mathrm{tr}\left[ (U^{-1} dU)^3 \right] \in \mathbb{Z}\]

This is the baryon number as a winding number. For configurations
homotopic to the identity (the vacuum), B = 0. For Skyrmions, B = 1 (the
proton and neutron) or higher.

We define the \textbf{topological invariant} \(I_w\) of a hadronic
configuration by its value of B:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \(I_w = 0\) : field configuration homotopic to the vacuum (no Skyrmion
  character)
\item
  \(I_w = n \in \mathbb{Z},\, n \neq 0\) : topological soliton with
  baryon number n
\end{itemize}

For the meson, tetraquark, and pentaquark (all with zero net baryon
number): \(I_w = 0\). For the baryon: \(I_w = 1\). This assignment is
uncontroversial in the Skyrme / Wess-Zumino-Witten framework and is a
consequence of the field topology, not of the assumed binding model.

\emph{Correspondence between descriptions.} The Skyrme model uses meson
fields with no explicit quarks; the stoichiometric classification of
§2.2 uses colour-singlet operator structure. These are two effective
descriptions of the same QCD. Their compatibility is established by the
large-\(N_c\) limit of QCD (Witten 1979 {[}5{]}): at leading order in
\(1/N_c\), QCD meson dynamics is captured by a local effective meson
theory, and baryons emerge from it as topological solitons (Adkins,
Nappi, Witten 1983 {[}4{]}). The quark model description emerges at
subleading order. The invariant \(I_w\) is compatible across both
descriptions at leading order in \(1/N_c\); the corrections are
\(O(1/N_c) \approx 30\%\) at physical \(N_c = 3\). We treat both
descriptions as complementary perspectives whose topological and
stoichiometric invariants agree in the overlap regime.

\textbf{The rigidity of \(I_w\).} Because \(B \in \mathbb{Z}\), the
topological invariant cannot change under continuous deformation of the
field configuration. A configuration with \(I_w = 1\) cannot be
continuously deformed to one with \(I_w = 0\) without passing through a
non-integer intermediate value --- which is excluded by the integer
quantization. This rigidity is absolute: it is not protected by an
energy barrier but by the topological structure of the field space. A
configuration cannot \emph{become} topologically trivial without a
discontinuous field event (an instanton). This is the mathematical
content of baryon stability, stated without any reference to baryon
number as an independent conserved quantity.

\hypertarget{stoichiometric-type}{%
\subsubsection{Stoichiometric Type}\label{stoichiometric-type}}

Within the space of configurations with a given \(I_w\), hadronic
configurations are further classified by the stoichiometric type of
their minimal gauge-invariant interpolating operator. We use operator
language throughout to avoid the gauge-dependence that attaches to
``valence quark number'' as a concept in QCD. The stoichiometric type is
a property of the operator structure, not of a particular gauge-fixed
quark description.

\textbf{Type U (unit balance).} The minimal gauge-invariant
colour-singlet interpolating operator for this configuration is a
product of quark-antiquark bilinears: \((\bar{q}\text{{\igprimfont ɢ}} q)^n\) for some
integer n.~The meson (n = 1) is the minimal instance; the tetraquark (n
= 2) is the next. Both are interpolated by operators built entirely from
\((\bar{q}\text{{\igprimfont ɢ}} q)\) factors; no baryon determinant appears. The
structural condition is that the operator admits a factorisation into
equal numbers of quark and antiquark field operators, grouped into
\((\bar{q}q)\) pairs.

\textbf{Type B (baryon type).} The minimal gauge-invariant
colour-singlet interpolating operator contains the fully antisymmetric
baryon determinant \(\varepsilon_{abc}\,q^a q^b q^c\). No antiquark
bilinear is required to form the colour singlet. The qqq baryon is the
instance; its minimal interpolating operator is one baryon determinant.

\textbf{Type A (asymmetric).} The minimal gauge-invariant colour-singlet
interpolating operator requires both a baryon determinant and at least
one quark-antiquark bilinear:
\((\varepsilon_{abc}\,q^a q^b q^c)(\bar{q}\text{{\igprimfont ɢ}} q)^n\) for some
\(n \geq 1\). The pentaquark qqqqq̄ is the instance: its minimal
colour-singlet operator is one baryon determinant (three quarks
colour-antisymmetrised) combined with one antiquark bilinear for the
remaining qq̄ content. This structure is irreducible --- no product of
type-U bilinears alone produces a baryon determinant, and no type-B
operator produces the antiquark required.

These three types are pairwise exclusive and, for the hadrons under
consideration, exhaustive. No hadronic configuration belongs to more
than one. The operator-based definitions are gauge-invariant by
construction.

\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

\hypertarget{the-structural-composition-principle}{%
\subsection{The Structural Composition
Principle}\label{the-structural-composition-principle}}

The central principle of this paper is the following:

\textbf{Principle (Structural Composition).} A hadronic configuration H
is \emph{structurally new} if and only if it does not share both
invariants (\(I_w\) and stoichiometric type) with any simpler
configuration M. If H shares both invariants with M, then H is not a new
structural species: it is a composite of M, and all physical properties
of H that differ from those of M (mass, width, decay modes, binding
energy) are kinematic, not structural.

This principle is not a new dynamical assumption. It is the statement
that class --- as determined by field topology and quark
balance --- is the correct and complete basis for determining what
counts as a new particle species. Kinematic differences between
configurations in the same structural class (such as the mass gap
between the meson and the X(3872)) are real and physically important,
but they are properties of the \emph{composite}, not of the
\emph{class}.

The principle is the particle-physics analogue of the following
mathematical fact: two vector bundles over the same base space with the
same structure group and the same topological characteristic classes are
isomorphic as bundles, regardless of the choice of connection. The
connection data (curvature, holonomy) may differ; the bundle structure
is the same. A hadronic configuration that differs from a meson pair
only in its connection data (binding energy, decay width) is a meson
pair.

\emph{Status of the Principle.} The Structural Composition Principle is
an organising criterion, not a theorem derived from QCD. The empirical
case is primary: every known type-U, \(I_w = 0\) state appears near a
di-meson threshold, and no confirmed state has been found far below
threshold where compact-object binding on the QCD scale would be
required. Theoretical support comes from the large-\(N_c\) limit (Witten
1979 {[}5{]}): at leading order in \(1/N_c\), compact multiquark binding
in the type-U sector is parametrically suppressed, consistent with this
empirical near-threshold dominance; any compact tetraquark pole emerges
only at subleading order, where \(1/N_c \approx 0.33\) at physical
\(N_c = 3\) means corrections are not negligible. The Principle
extrapolates the large-\(N_c\) result to physical \(N_c = 3\). It is an
empirically motivated extrapolation, not a derivation.

\hypertarget{the-tetraquark-is-not-an-exotic-hadron}{%
\subsection{The Tetraquark Is Not an Exotic
Hadron}\label{the-tetraquark-is-not-an-exotic-hadron}}

The tetraquark qqq̄q̄ has:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \(I_w = 0\) (zero baryon number, no topological winding --- identical
  to the meson)
\item
  Stoichiometric type U (two quarks, two antiquarks in equal measure ---
  the colour-singlet decomposition pairs them as (qq̄)(qq̄), each pair of
  type U --- identical to the meson's type)
\end{itemize}

By the Structural Composition Principle, the tetraquark is not
structurally new. It is a composite of two mesons. No new exotic
hadronic species exists with the quantum numbers of the tetraquark; what
exists is a di-meson molecule.

This conclusion is not contingent on any dynamical model. It follows
from the observation that:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  The tetraquark has the same topological charge as the meson (both
  zero). This is accepted by every particle physicist.
\item
  The tetraquark's quark-antiquark balance --- two quarks paired with
  two antiquarks --- is the same type as the meson's
  quark-antiquark balance --- one quark paired with one antiquark. The
  \emph{type} of the pairing is the same; only the \emph{multiplicity}
  differs. A physicist who denies that two equal-ratio pairs have the
  same stoichiometric type as one equal-ratio pair is denying that 2 ×
  (1:1) = (1:1) at the structural level.
\item
  Therefore the tetraquark and the meson share both invariants. The Structural Composition Principle then applies
  directly.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{On threshold criticality.} All tetraquark candidates (X(3872),
Zc(3900), etc.) are observed within a few MeV of specific di-meson
thresholds. This is the kinematic signature of a composite at the
boundary of its own dissociation: the system is a meson pair weakly
bound near threshold, and near-threshold states are critical --- their
correlation length diverges. This critical signature is a consequence of
the compositional structure, not evidence against it. The tetraquark
signals its own compositional nature through its threshold proximity.

The X(3872) has a binding energy of less than 0.2 MeV relative to the
D⁰D̄*⁰ threshold (3871.81 MeV). This is the energy scale of van der Waals
colour forces between two mesons --- not of a compact four-quark object,
which would be bound on the scale of QCD (hundreds of MeV). The
structural classification and the binding energy are consistent.

\textbf{Fully charmed tetraquark confirmation.} The LHCb Collaboration
observed structure in the J/ψ-pair invariant mass distribution in 2020
{[}7{]}, including the prominent X(6900) near 6.9 GeV. As a ccc̄c̄
configuration, it has \(I_w = 0\) and stoichiometric type U: two charm
quarks paired against two charm antiquarks in equal measure. The fully
charmed sector is a structurally clean test because no hadronic molecule
objection based on quark-flavor miscibility applies --- all four
constituents carry the same flavor --- yet the state appears
approximately 35 MeV above the J/ψψ(3770) di-charmonium threshold at
6.87 GeV, consistent with Prediction 1. The flavour degeneracy strips
away the dynamical complexity that complicates the light-quark
candidates and exposes the structural verdict directly: \(I_w = 0\),
type U, threshold proximity confirmed.

\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

\hypertarget{the-pentaquark-is-a-new-structural-class}{%
\subsection{The Pentaquark Is a New Structural
Class}\label{the-pentaquark-is-a-new-structural-class}}

The pentaquark qqqqq̄ has:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \(I_w = 0\) (zero net winding --- identical to the meson and
  tetraquark)
\item
  Stoichiometric type A (4:1 quark/antiquark ratio --- not decomposable
  into type-U pairs)
\end{itemize}

The stoichiometric type is the key. Four quarks and one antiquark cannot
be decomposed into any number of type-U (equal-ratio) pairs while
preserving the 4:1 ratio. To see this: any set of n type-U pairs
contributes n quarks and n antiquarks to the colour singlet; the
resulting structure has net quark- antiquark ratio 1:1, not 4:1. No
composition of type-U pairs produces type A.

Similarly, the pentaquark is not type B (baryon type), because type B
requires no antiquarks in the binding role; the pentaquark's lone
antiquark is present in the binding structure.

The pentaquark therefore occupies a stoichiometric class distinct from
both the meson and the baryon. By the Structural Composition Principle,
it is genuinely new --- it cannot be expressed as a composite of any
previously classified hadronic configuration.

\emph{What structural novelty means.} The classification of the
pentaquark as a new class is definitional: it follows from
the stoichiometric type, which follows from the quark content and the
decomposition structure. Like any principled taxonomy, the value of the
structural assignment lies not in the label but in what the label
entails. What is empirically significant is the predicted decay pattern.
Because the pentaquark has \(I_w = 0\) with stoichiometric type A, the
baryon component (\(I_w = 1\)) is the structural anchor from which the
composite cannot be separated by a continuous process; the meson
component (\(I_w = 0\)) can dissociate freely. The classification
therefore predicts --- without dynamical input --- that all pentaquark
decay modes must pass through a baryon-meson channel, with the baryon
remaining stable. The observed Pc states in J/ψ-proton are the
experimental expression of this structural constraint.

\emph{Scope of the classification.} The framework extends beyond the
four configurations discussed. A dibaryon (qqq qqq) has \(I_w = 2\) and
type B; it is a class distinct from the single baryon, and
the deuteron is the lightest experimental example. Higher multiquark
configurations generalise straightforwardly. This paper focuses on the
four configurations at the centre of the exotic hadron debate; the
classification is not limited to them.

The dibaryon / deuteron case is structurally instructive by contrast
with the tetraquark. In both cases two copies of a simpler object are
combined, but the topological invariant behaves differently in each.
Topological winding is additive under composition of colour-singlet
configurations that do not share valence quarks --- this follows from
the homotopy class of the product field configuration, established at
large \(N_c\) in Witten 1979 {[}5{]} and approximately valid at physical
\(N_c = 3\). For type-U composition (meson paired with meson), \(I_w\)
is additive and zero is preserved: \(I_w = 0 + 0 = 0\), identical to the
single meson. The composite lands in the same class as its
constituents, which is precisely why the tetraquark is not structurally
new --- combination does not escape the class. For type-B
composition (baryon paired with baryon), \(I_w\) is again additive but
the initial value is non-zero: \(I_w = 1 + 1 = 2\), which differs from
the single baryon's \(I_w = 1\). The dibaryon therefore occupies a
distinct structural class (\(I_w = 2\), type B), with the deuteron as
its empirical ground state. The \(I_w\) arithmetic is the mechanism:
whether composition generates a new structural class depends entirely on
whether the topological invariant is preserved --- zero winding is
preserved under composition, non-zero winding accumulates.

\textbf{However}, the pentaquark has \(I_w = 0\): it is not
topologically protected. Structural novelty and topological stability
are orthogonal properties. The pentaquark is a new class that
is dynamically unstable because its field configuration has no
topological protection against decay. Its decay products must conserve
baryon number (\(I_w = 1\) for the proton component), which is why all
observed pentaquarks appear in the J/ψ-proton channel: the baryon
component (the proton, \(I_w = 1\)) does not decay, while the mesonic
component (\(I_w = 0\)) does. The structural classification predicts
this decay pattern without appealing to any model of the pentaquark's
internal dynamics.

\hypertarget{the-baryons-stability-is-topological-not-energetic}{%
\subsection{The Baryon's Stability Is Topological, Not
Energetic}\label{the-baryons-stability-is-topological-not-energetic}}

The baryon has \(I_w = 1\): its pion field wraps once around the
three-sphere. This winding is not protected by an energy barrier. It is
protected by topology.

The argument is elementary. To transform a configuration with
\(I_w = 1\) into one with \(I_w = 0\) by a continuous field deformation,
the winding number would have to pass through a non-integer value during
the deformation. But \(B \in \mathbb{Z}\): baryon number is the degree
of a continuous map between compact manifolds, and degrees of continuous
maps are integers. No continuous deformation can move the degree from 1
to 0 without passing through a non-integer intermediate --- which is
excluded by the discrete nature of homotopy classes. A discontinuous
field event (an instanton) would be required.

At the energy scales of accelerator physics and below, no such instanton
amplitude has been observed. The proton does not decay because the
topological invariant \(I_w = 1\) is a discrete label, not a continuous
parameter. Baryon number conservation is the experimental statement of
this topological rigidity.

Two consequences follow:

\textbf{Consequence 7.1.} Within QCD (strong interactions alone), proton
decay is topologically forbidden: no perturbative or non-perturbative
QCD process can change \(I_w\). In the full Standard Model, electroweak
sphalerons can change baryon number by discontinuously changing the
\(\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\) Chern-Simons number --- this is the content of the
sphaleron argument in §9, and it is why baryon asymmetry in the early
universe is possible. The topological protection claimed here is
therefore a statement about the QCD sector at hadronic energy scales,
not about the full electroweak theory. At energies far below \(M_W\),
electroweak instanton amplitudes are suppressed by
\(\exp(-8\pi^2/g^2) \approx 10^{-165}\) and play no role in hadronic
spectroscopy. Grand unified proton decay via gauge boson exchange is a
separate, electroweak-scale process governed by GUT physics, not by the
hadronic \(I_w\) invariant.

\textbf{Consequence 7.2.} Whatever effective description of the baryon
is adopted, it must reproduce the topological sector that assigns
\(I_w = 1\) to the baryon. The Skyrme / WZW description captures this
sector explicitly. Other effective frameworks --- large-N QCD,
holographic duals, AdS/QCD models --- also reproduce non-trivial
baryonic winding in their respective formalisms. The model details are
framework-dependent; the topological sector they must share is not. A
description that fails to assign \(I_w = 1\) to the baryon fails to
encode its invariant and therefore cannot correctly account
for baryon stability, regardless of its other merits.

\textbf{Prediction 4.} No four-quark configuration has \(I_w \neq 0\).

\textbf{Prediction 5.} Future exotic hadronic configurations will belong
to one of the three classes (U, B, A) or to their
generalisations at higher quark multiplicity, each with an associated
\(I_w\). No new class can appear below the scale at which
non-perturbative instanton effects modify the field topology.

\hypertarget{discussion}{%
\subsection{Discussion}\label{discussion}}

The central claim of this paper is that class membership ---
determined by (\(I_w\), stoichiometric type) --- is the appropriate
basis for deciding whether a hadronic configuration is new. The
dynamical question of how the configuration binds is secondary; it
determines the kinematic properties (mass, width) of the configuration,
not its identity.

This distinction matters for the interpretation of exotic hadron
searches. The experimental programme has been asking: ``is the X(3872) a
compact tetraquark or a di-meson molecule?'' The Structural Composition
Principle renders this question a false dichotomy. There is no
structural difference between a compact tetraquark and a di-meson
molecule if both have \(I_w = 0\) and stoichiometric type U. They are
the same structural object in different kinematic presentations. The
``compact tetraquark'' and the ``di-meson molecule'' are two names for
one class, differentiated only by the assumed internal
binding mechanism --- which is a kinematic, not a structural, question.

The correct question is: does the configuration have structural
invariants that distinguish it from known configurations? For the
tetraquark, the answer is no. For the pentaquark, the answer is yes. For
the baryon, the topological invariant is what makes it distinct.

\textbf{On completeness.} One might object that (\(I_w\), stoichiometric
type) does not exhaust the structural information in a hadronic
configuration --- that isospin, spin, or charge are equally invariant
under continuous field deformations and therefore equally structural.
The objection requires a sharp criterion for what separates an invariant from a kinematic quantum number. We propose two criteria, each
sufficient.

\emph{The flavor-permutation criterion.} An invariant is one
that is invariant under arbitrary permutation of quark flavors (swapping
u↔d, replacing light quarks with strange or charm content) while
preserving the colour-singlet structure. Stoichiometric type satisfies
this: the quark/antiquark ratio is unchanged by flavor substitution.
\(I_w\) satisfies this: the homotopy class of the field configuration is
unchanged by flavor substitution. Isospin does not satisfy this:
swapping u↔d changes the isospin label. Electric charge does not:
replacing a u quark with a d quark changes the charge. Strangeness and
charm do not. Spin requires separate treatment because it is, in fact,
flavor-blind: replacing all u quarks with d quarks in a spin-1 meson
gives a spin-1 meson. Spin therefore passes the flavor-permutation
criterion and must be disqualified on different grounds. The relevant
criterion here is that an invariant must be constant across
all members of the same class. Spin is not: the type-U,
\(I_w = 0\) class contains spin-0++, spin-1++, and spin-2++
di-meson composites simultaneously --- the same constituent mesons,
coupled differently. Spin is an angular-momentum label on the state; it
depends on how the constituents' spins are aligned, not on the topology
of the field configuration or the stoichiometric type. Class
is determined before spin coupling is specified. Spin labels states
within a class, exactly as different hydrogen orbitals are states within
the structural class ``hydrogen atom,'' not different structural
classes.

\emph{The topological vs.~Noether criterion.} The invariant \(I_w\) is a
topological charge --- a homotopy invariant that classifies the global
structure of the field configuration space. It cannot be changed by any
local field operation; changing it requires a non-perturbative, globally
non-trivial event (an instanton). Isospin is a Noether charge: it is
associated with the approximate \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\) isospin symmetry of
the Lagrangian and is generated by a conserved current. Noether charges
can be changed by symmetry-breaking interactions (isospin is broken by
electromagnetic effects and by quark mass differences) or by operating
with the isospin ladder operators. They classify representations of a
symmetry group, not the topology of field configuration space. These are
categorically different kinds of invariant: topological charges describe
what the configuration \emph{is} globally; Noether charges describe how
it \emph{transforms} under a symmetry.

A potential objection: baryon number is itself the Noether charge of the
global \(U(1)_B\) symmetry of the Standard Model Lagrangian, so isn't
\(I_w\) also a Noether charge by identification? The answer is that
\(I_w\) is not the Noether charge of \(U(1)_B\) --- it is the
topological quantity that the Noether charge counts at low energy. The
two agree numerically for QCD bound states in the absence of electroweak
effects, but they are categorically distinct in their protection
mechanism. The distinction is demonstrated by the electroweak sphaleron:
sphaleron processes violate the \(U(1)_B\) Noether charge (they change B
by ±3) through topological tunneling in the electroweak sector. They do
so precisely because they constitute a discontinuous change in
topological winding number --- the instanton number of the
\(\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\) gauge field changes by one unit, and \(U(1)_B\)
violation follows. The Noether charge can be violated; \(I_w\) changes
only through non-perturbative topology changes. If baryon number
conservation were purely a Noether charge, it would be violated
perturbatively by sphaleron-like processes at high enough temperature
--- and in the early universe it is, which is why baryon asymmetry is
possible. The structural invariant in this paper is \(I_w\): the
topological realization of what baryon number physically is in the
hadronic sector, independent of the \(U(1)_B\) Noether charge and its
electroweak fate. To forestall a further objection: the
\(\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\) instanton number that changes in sphaleron
processes and the hadronic Skyrmion winding \(I_w\) are formally
distinct topological objects in different gauge sectors --- the former
is the Chern-Simons number of the electroweak gauge field; the latter is
the degree of the map from physical three-space to \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\)
defined by the pion field. They are connected by the ABJ anomaly
(\(U(1)_B\) violation is proportional to the change in
\(\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\) Chern-Simons number) but are not the same
mathematical object. Both serve the same illustrative purpose here: each
demonstrates independently that topological protection and Noether
charge conservation are distinct mechanisms, operating in different
sectors through different mathematical structures.

Together, these criteria give a principled demarcation. The invariants (\(I_w\), stoichiometric type) are flavor-blind and, in the
case of \(I_w\), topological. The kinematic quantum numbers (isospin,
charge, spin, strangeness) are either flavor-sensitive or label states
within a structural class. No candidate structural invariant --- in the
sense of either criterion --- has been exhibited that distinguishes the
tetraquark from the di-meson configuration.

An equivalent formulation: structural invariants classify \emph{sectors}
of the theory --- the topological sector (indexed by \(I_w\)) and the
stoichiometric sector (indexed by operator type). Spin and isospin are
quantum numbers of \emph{states} within a sector. The pion triplet (π+,
π0, π−) confirms this: all three are \(I_w = 0\), type U --- they occupy
one class, and isospin labels their states within it. The
classification does not separate the pion triplet, as it should not;
their identity is the same, and their isospin quantum numbers
are a consequence of flavor content within that class.

\textbf{On the charged Z states.} The charged states Zc(3900) and
Zb(10650) appeared to require compact tetraquarks because a charged cc̄
or bb̄ meson is impossible. The classification resolves this: the
stoichiometric type U does not count electric charge; it counts
structural role types (quark vs.~antiquark) in the colour-singlet
decomposition. A (D⁺)(D̄*⁰) molecule has two quarks and two antiquarks in
total, with the charge residing on the D⁺ component. Its type
is U; its charge is a kinematic property of one constituent. No new
structural class is required.

\hypertarget{conclusion}{%
\subsection{Conclusion}\label{conclusion}}

Three physical facts, none of which is controversial, determine the
classification of all four hadronic configurations:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\item
  Mesons, tetraquarks, and pentaquarks have zero topological winding
  number. Baryons have non-zero integer winding number.
\item
  The tetraquark's colour-singlet decomposition has the same
  stoichiometric type as the meson: equal quark and antiquark structural
  contributions, in pairs.
\item
  The baryon's integer winding number cannot change by a continuous
  field deformation. Integer degrees of maps between compact manifolds
  are discrete.
\end{enumerate}

From these three facts and the Structural Composition Principle, the
classification follows necessarily:

\begin{itemize}
\item
  The tetraquark is a di-meson composite. The experimental observation
  that all tetraquark candidates appear near di-meson thresholds is the
  kinematic confirmation of this structural verdict.
\item
  The pentaquark is a new class --- not because of its quark
  count, but because its stoichiometric type (4:1) is indecomposable
  into equal-ratio pairs.
\item
  The baryon is topologically protected, and baryon number conservation
  is the empirical manifestation of the discrete nature of the
  topological invariant. It is not an independent postulate; it is a
  consequence of the field topology.
\end{itemize}

The classification does not require QCD to be solved. It requires that
the topological and stoichiometric facts about hadronic configurations
be taken seriously as the basis for identity.

The approach taken here --- seeking invariants that cut through
dynamical detail --- follows a principle articulated by Larson {[}8{]}:
identify what is structurally true, then follow where that truth leads
you.
