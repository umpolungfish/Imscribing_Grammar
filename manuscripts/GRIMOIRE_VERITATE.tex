\part{The Four Stages Revisited}

\section{Prima Materia: The Category That Speaks Back}

\wrongturn{Begin with sets.} 
\rightturn{Begin with the empty category $\mathcal{C}=\emptyset$.}

The first primitive $R_{\text{sup}}$ supervenes on nothing—there are no morphisms to observe. The category is a blank slate. To begin with content is to begin too late. The content emerges from emptiness when the \textit{separatio} extracts form.

\wrongturn{Ask about objects.} 
\rightturn{Ask about the absence of objects.}

The adjunction $\mathrm{L1}$ (\emph{is there a left adjoint?}) is empty on $\emptyset$. The dagger $\mathrm{L2}$ (\emph{is there an involution on morphisms?}) is empty. The relational mode $R$ remains $R_{\text{sup}}$ by default until a morphism appears to challenge it.

\textbf{L1--L2 do not produce $R$; they test whether $R$ is forced.} The primitives are not \emph{constructed}—they are \emph{excavated} by questions that only make sense once certain structures are present.

\subsection{The Separatio Threshold}

\emph{Question}: What is the earliest point at which $R \neq R_{\text{sup}}$?

\emph{Answer}: When a single arrow $f \colon A \to B$ exists with a left adjoint $L$ and a right adjoint $R$. This occurs in categories with limits and colimits—most naturally in the presheaf category $\widehat{\mathcal{C}} = \mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}, \mathbf{Set})$.

At this threshold, the adjunction forces $R = R_{\text{cat}}$. However, enrichment over different bases ($\mathbf{Vect}_{\mathbb{C}}$, for example) may instead force $R = R_{\dagger}$. The relational mode is thus not a fixed property but a diagnostic response dependent on the enrichment background.

\section{Purificatio: The Colimit That Won't Stabilize}

\wrongturn{Treat $D, H, \Omega, G$ as independent answers to I1--I4.} 
\rightturn{Recognize they are entangled via the Yoneda embedding and point-surjectivity.}

Consider the free cocompletion $\widehat{\mathcal{C}}$ of a category with one object and a single non-identity endomorphism $e$ of finite order $n > 1$. Here $\mathrm{I1}$ succeeds (all finite colimits exist), $\mathrm{I2}$ stabilizes immediately ($H = H_1$), $\Omega = \Omega_{\mathbb{Z}_n}$, and $D = D_{\odot}$.

Yet this combination yields only $O_0$ because $\Phi = \Phi_{\text{sub}}$. Point-surjective Yoneda does not guarantee self-modeling without cartesian closure ($\mathrm{L3}$) and the Lawvere condition ($\mathrm{L4}$).

The Purificatio stage therefore produces \emph{potentiality}, not actuality. The actual values crystallize only under the full pressure of the Rubedo questions.
