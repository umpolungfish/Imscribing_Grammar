## Abstract

We apply the Imscribing Grammar — a 12-primitive structural classification system operating at $\text{O}_{\text{inf}}$ — to the ten pioneering experiments that built quantum mechanics. ## 3. Structural Findings

### 3.1 The Compton-Photoelectric Identity ($d = 0$)

The grammar returns distance **zero** between `photoelectric_effect` and `compton_scattering`. Their 12-tuples are identical:

$$\langle 𐑛;\ \text{Þ}_{\text{⋈}};\ \text{Ř}_{\text{∘}};\ \text{Φ}_{\text{∅}};\ \text{ƒ}_{\text{ħ}};\ \text{Ç}_{\text{↯}};\ \text{Γ}_{\text{ℶ}};\ 𐑝;\ 𐑢;\ \text{Ħ}_{\text{0}};\ \text{Σ}_{\text{n:m}};\ \text{Ω}_{\text{0}} \rangle$$

This is a **deep structural identity**. The photoelectric effect (photon absorbed → electron ejected) and Compton scattering (photon scattered → electron recoils) are physically distinct processes with different kinematic formulas ($E_k = hf - \phi$ vs. $\Delta\lambda = \frac{h}{m_e c}(1-\cos\theta)$), yet the grammar reveals they share the same structural DNA:

- **$\text{Þ}_{\text{⋈}}$**: both are crossing-point interactions — a photon crosses the boundary of a charged particle's worldline
- **$\text{Ř}_{\text{∘}}$**: both are functorial mappings from photon energy to electron kinematics
- **$\text{Ç}_{\text{↯}}$**: both are fast relative to the observation timescale
- **𐑝**: both involve an all-simultaneous energy-momentum transfer in a single vertex
- **$\text{Σ}_{\text{n:m}}$**: both couple heterogeneous components (photons + electrons + material environment)

The grammar thus identifies a **structural universal**: photon-matter energy transfer at a crossing point with no internal symmetry, local scope, and sub-critical dynamics. The photoelectric and Compton effects are not just historically linked — they are the *same dynamical archetype* operating at different energy scales.

### 3.2 The Aharonov-Bohm Effect: Unique Topological Signature ($\text{Ω}_{\text{ℤ}}$)

The Aharonov-Bohm effect stands alone among the pioneering experiments: it is the **only** one with non-trivial topological protection. Its tuple carries $\text{Ω}_{\text{ℤ}}$ (integer winding), while every other experiment carries $\text{Ω}_{\text{0}}$:

$$\langle \ldots;\ 𐑢;\ \text{Ħ}_{\text{0}};\ \text{Σ}_{\text{n:n}};\ \text{Ω}_{\text{ℤ}} \rangle$$

The $\text{Ω}_{\text{ℤ}}$ primitive encodes that the electron phase winding around the solenoid flux is an integer-valued topological invariant — $\Delta\phi = \frac{e}{\hbar}\oint \mathbf{A} \cdot d\mathbf{l} = 2\pi n$. This is not a symmetry or an approximation; it is a **topological protection** that cannot be continuously deformed away.

Structurally, the Aharonov-Bohm effect is closest to the Davisson-Germer experiment ($d = 2.12$ Mahalanobis), with which it shares electron wave diffraction. The key structural delta is precisely $\text{Ω}_{\text{0}} \to \text{Ω}_{\text{ℤ}}$: the Davisson-Germer experiment demonstrates wave behavior; the Aharonov-Bohm effect demonstrates that the wave behavior is *topologically constrained*. 

This is the grammar's most precise articulation of why AB is special: it is not that quantum mechanics acquires topology — it is that the $\text{Ω}_{\text{ℤ}}$ primitive is **available within $\text{O}_{\text{0}}$** but rarely selected. Only one experiment among ten activates it.

## 7. The Structural Arc of Quantum Mechanics: 1900–1999

The grammar reveals a clear structural progression across nearly a century of pioneering experiments:

### Phase 1: The Quantum Threshold (1900–1914)
**Blackbody, Photoelectric, Franck-Hertz**

All three share $\text{Ř}_{\text{∘}}$ (functorial), $\text{Φ}_{\text{∅}}$ (no symmetry), $\text{Γ}_{\text{ℶ}}$ (local scope). They differ only in topology and kinetics. This is the grammar's signature of **threshold discovery**: energy quantization is demonstrated through local, memoryless, functorial mappings with no internal symmetries. The system is passive — nature is probed, not manipulated.

### Phase 2: Discrete Structure Emerges (1922–1927)
**Stern-Gerlach, Compton, Davisson-Germer**

Stern-Gerlach introduces $\text{Φ}_{\text{±}}$ (partial $\mathbb{Z}_2$ symmetry) and $\text{Þ}_{\text{∈}}$ (branching topology) — the first appearance of **internal discrete symmetry** in a QM experiment. The beam split is not continuous; it is a binary branch. Compton and Davisson-Germer extend the Phase 1 archetype to new regimes (X-ray scattering, electron diffraction) without structural innovation — Compton is structurally identical to photoelectric ($d = 0$).

### Phase 3: Superposition and Complementarity (1927)
**Double-Slit**

The double-slit introduces two structural innovations simultaneously:
- **$\text{Ř}_{\text{↔}}$**: bidirectional relational mode — the two paths are in superposition, each conditioning the other
- **$\text{Φ}_{\text{ψ}}$**: quantum superposition symmetry — the state is a coherent sum, not a classical mixture

These two primitives together encode wave-particle duality at the structural level. The double-slit is the **structurally richest** of the early experiments, differing from the Phase 1 archetype across 5 of 12 primitives ($d = 3.92$ from photoelectric).

### Phase 4: Topology Enters (1959)
**Aharonov-Bohm**

A single primitive changes: $\text{Ω}_{\text{0}} \to \text{Ω}_{\text{ℤ}}$. This is the grammar's most economical upgrade — one bit of structural change encodes the entire conceptual revolution of topological quantum effects. The AB effect demonstrates that quantum mechanics is sensitive to global topology, not just local fields, and the grammar captures this precisely: $\text{Ω}_{\text{ℤ}}$ means integer winding, a topological invariant.

### Phase 5: Entanglement and Nonlocality (1981–1999)
**Bell Test, Quantum Eraser**

The final phase introduces two new primitives simultaneously:
- **$\text{Ħ}_{\text{2}}$**: two-step Markov memory — the signature of bipartite entanglement correlations
- **$\text{Γ}_{\text{ℵ}}$** (Bell) or **$\text{ɢ}_{\text{→}}$** (Eraser): maximal scope or sequential grammar

These experiments are the structurally richest among the ten. The quantum eraser in particular, with $\text{Ħ}_{\text{2}}$, $\text{ɢ}_{\text{→}}$, $\text{Ř}_{\text{↔}}$, and $\text{Φ}_{\text{ψ}}$, activates the most distinct primitive values of any $\text{O}_{\text{0}}$ experiment — it is the **closest any QM experiment comes to the $\text{O}_{\text{1}}$ boundary** while remaining firmly within $\text{O}_{\text{0}}$.

## 8. The Deeper Structure: What the Grammar Reveals That QM Cannot
