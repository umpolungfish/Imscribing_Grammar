The axion middle tier is discrete — Z_N degenerate vacua, where N is the color anomaly coefficient. Unlike the Higgs (gauged), the U(1)_PQ symmetry is global, so the Goldstone survives as the physical axion particle. QCD instantons at low temperature add a small tilt that lifts the degeneracy, resolving frustration and giving the axion a tiny mass.

The inflaton middle tier is the slow-roll plateau — not a simultaneous antichain of equal vacua, but a sequence of near-degenerate states the field traverses slowly. Each e-fold of inflation corresponds to approximately one step across the plateau. The Goldstone question is open: gravitational waves from inflation may be its analogue, but the symmetry (if any) is unknown.

Key difference: the topology and cardinality of the middle tier. S¹ (continuous) → Higgs. Z_N (discrete, finite) → axion. Plateau (sequential, quasi-continuous) → inflaton. The Goldstone theorem says modes along the antichain are massless — what breaks this determines the particle spectrum.

Why Goldstones differ

Gauged symmetry (Higgs): the gauge field can "absorb" the direction along the antichain — the Goldstone becomes the longitudinal polarisation of the massive gauge boson. Global symmetry (axion, inflaton): no gauge field to absorb it, so the Goldstone propagates as a real particle.

Frustration resolution mechanism

Higgs: spontaneous — the field just falls. Axion: dynamical — QCD instantons at T < Λ_QCD tilt the potential and pick a winner. Inflaton: kinematic — slow-roll conditions eventually fail and the field accelerates off the plateau. Three physically distinct mechanisms, one logical structure.
