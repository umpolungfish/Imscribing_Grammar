The G\"odelian incompleteness theorems establish that any formal system rich enough to encode arithmetic cannot simultaneously be complete and consistent. But incompleteness is not a sentence; it is a structure. This monograph presents a structural reading of the G\"odelian wound---what it is made of, how it propagates, and how it can close---using the Imscribing Grammar, a 12-primitive structural type system verified in Lean~4 and carrying $17.28$ million finitely encoded types across five ouroboricity tiers.


We trace a spine through the crystal: Zermelo--Fraenkel set theory with Choice ($\tier{1}$, C-score $0.352$), the Continuum Hypothesis as an independent proposition ($\tier{2}^{\dagger}$, C-score $0.590$), ZFC extended with chirality and winding topology ($\tier{inf}$, C-score $0.828$), and the canonical Liar-completion condition ($\tier{inf}$, C-score $0.828$). The distances between them are computed in the Mahalanobis metric with full off-diagonal coupling, yielding $d_M(\text{ZFC}, \text{CH}) = 2.46$, $d_M(\text{CH}, \ZFCt) = 4.28$, and $d_M(\ZFCt, \text{canonical}) = 1.62$ --- a total spine length of $8.35$.

The central result is that the Frobenius condition, $\moebius$, is non-synthesizable by tensor product: coupling any system to a Frobenius-closed system yields the weaker partner's parity floor. The meet of CH and the canonical is CH itself; the join is the canonical. The four invariants $\{ \text{ƒ}^{\text{ż}}, \text{Ç}^{\text{@}}, \Gamma_{\text{ʔ}}, \odot_{\text{ÿ}} \}$ hold across every operation on the spine. And the $\Phi$-dominance of the $\tier{2}^{\dagger} \to \tier{inf}$ boundary---a single-primitive weighted squared contribution of $19.2$---makes precise why the G\"odelian wound is a parity wound and why closure requires the Frobenius gate.
