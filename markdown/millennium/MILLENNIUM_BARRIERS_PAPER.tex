\subsection{I. Introduction (v0.1.0, 2026-03-26)}

The Clay Mathematics Institute seven Millennium Prize Problems have resisted proof for decades  --  and in one case, the Riemann Hypothesis, for over a century and a half. Proof assistants have formalized large bodies of mathematics, but existing efforts overwhelmingly target \emph{results that are known}: the Last Theorem of Fermat, the Four Color Theorem, the Kepler Conjecture. Far less attention has been paid to formalizing \emph{why specific problems are hard}  --  the structural barriers that distinguish them from merely difficult but open problems.

This paper presents a formal barrier taxonomy for all seven Millennium Problems in Lean 4. The contribution is meta-level: a machine-checked classification of the proof obligations, the structural relationships between them, and their connection to an underlying primitive constraint algebra. The taxonomy locates each problem precisely --- which is the prerequisite for directed proof search.

\subsubsection{I.1 Why this matters}

The distinction between barrier types has practical consequences for the formalization community.

\textbf{MathlibGap sorries are actionable.} A contributor with the right background can in principle discharge them by formalizing a known proof. The \texttt{euler\_opn\_form} sorry in OPN.lean (Euler 1747) and the \texttt{mazur\_torsion} sorry in BSD.lean (Mazur 1977) are of this type.

\textbf{OpenProblem sorries define the research frontier.} Discharging them requires solving the underlying mathematics --- each one marks that frontier with a typed proposition. Knowing which sorries are of this type  --  as opposed to merely MathlibGap  --  directs effort to the right layer.

\textbf{MissingFoundation sorries locate a deeper layer than OpenProblems.} An OpenProblem has a well-typed proposition whose truth value is to be determined. A MissingFoundation sorry locates the exact type that must be constructed --- the full question is statable once that type exists. Yang-Mills is the only Millennium Problem in this category. We prove this formally.

\subsubsection{I.2 The sorry depth distinction}

We introduce a formal notion of \emph{sorry depth} distinguishing \emph{stacked} from \emph{parallel} proof obligations.

\textbf{Stacked (Yang-Mills):} sorry $B$ depends on sorry $A$  --  the mass gap proposition is statable once the quantum Yang-Mills theory exists; discharging sorry $A$ (existence) unlocks sorry $B$ (mass gap).

\textbf{Parallel (BSD):} three sorries are logically independent  --  Mordell-Weil (proved 1922, MathlibGap), the Mazur torsion theorem (proved 1977, MathlibGap), and the BSD rank formula itself (OpenProblem)  --  each dischargeable independently.

Both Yang-Mills and BSD have \texttt{sorryDepth = 2}. The structural difference is encoded in the barrier type.

\subsubsection{I.3 Contributions}

\textbf{C1  --  BarrierType taxonomy:} A typed inductive with three constructors, formally distinct (by \texttt{decide}), and computably assigned to all seven Millennium Problems.

\subsubsection{II.2 The sorry in Lean}

In Lean 4, \texttt{sorry} is an axiom of type $\alpha$ for any $\alpha : \mathsf{Prop}$ (or any type). It allows a proof to typecheck despite containing unfinished obligations. A \texttt{sorry}-free proof is fully kernel-verified; a proof with \texttt{sorry} is verified modulo those axioms. There is no built-in mechanism in Lean to classify \emph{why} a sorry cannot be discharged. The \texttt{BarrierType} inductive in \pilcrow{}III provides this classification for the first time.

In the final library, each sorry boundary is declared as an \texttt{axiom} rather than left as a literal \texttt{sorry}:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=lean]
axiom rh_sorry_boundary : Millennium.RH.RiemannHypothesis
\end{lstlisting}

This is intentional. An \texttt{axiom} is explicit about its foundational status  --  it extends the logical framework at a named location rather than leaving an opaque hole.

\subsection{III. The Barrier Taxonomy (v0.1.0, 2026-03-26)}

\subsubsection{III.1 The BarrierType inductive}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=lean]
inductive BarrierType
  | MathlibGap
  | OpenProblem
  | MissingFoundation
  deriving Repr, DecidableEq
\end{lstlisting}

The three constructors represent qualitatively distinct epistemic states.

\textbf{\texttt{MathlibGap}:} The theorem has been proved in the mathematical literature. A Lean proof exists \emph{in principle} using currently available tools; the sorry persists only because no one has yet carried out the formalization. Such sorries are \emph{defeasible}  --  they will eventually be discharged as Mathlib grows. Examples: the Euler 1747 characterization of OPN structure (\texttt{euler\_opn\_form}); the Mazur 1977 torsion theorem (\texttt{mazur\_torsion}).

\textbf{\texttt{OpenProblem}:} The theorem is well-typed and precisely stated; discharging the sorry requires solving the underlying mathematics. Examples: the Riemann Hypothesis, the BSD rank conjecture, the nonexistence of odd perfect numbers.

\textbf{\texttt{MissingFoundation}:} The sorry locates a type that is yet to be constructed as a rigorous mathematical object. The distinction from \texttt{OpenProblem} is ontological: an \texttt{OpenProblem} has a well-typed proposition whose truth value is to be determined. A \texttt{MissingFoundation} sorry names the missing type precisely; the full proposition follows from constructing it. Example: \texttt{PathIntegralMeasure G} in Yang-Mills  --  the path integral measure over gauge connections modulo gauge equivalence in four Euclidean dimensions.

Formal distinctness is immediate:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=lean]
theorem missing_foundation_vs_open_problem :
    BarrierType.MissingFoundation != BarrierType.OpenProblem := by decide
\end{lstlisting}

\subsubsection{III.2 The master classification}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=lean]
def millenniumBarrier : MillenniumProblem -> BarrierType
  | .RH    => .OpenProblem
  | .Hodge => .OpenProblem
  | .PvsNP => .OpenProblem
  | .NS    => .OpenProblem
  | .YM    => .MissingFoundation
  | .BSD   => .OpenProblem
  | .OPN   => .OpenProblem
\end{lstlisting}

The central theorem of the library:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=lean]
theorem ym_is_unique_missing_foundation :
    forall p : MillenniumProblem,
      millenniumBarrier p = .MissingFoundation -> p = .YM := by
  intro p hp; cases p <;> simp_all [millenniumBarrier]
\end{lstlisting}

Yang-Mills is the only Millennium Problem with a \texttt{MissingFoundation} barrier. This is a theorem about the structure of mathematics, proved by exhaustive case analysis over the seven-constructor \texttt{MillenniumProblem} inductive, closed by \texttt{simp\_all}.

\subsubsection{III.3 Sorry depth}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=lean]
def sorryDepth : MillenniumProblem -> ℕ
  | .RH    => 1   -- ZeroFreeStrip 0 (one sorry; everything else proved)
  | .Hodge => 1   -- algebraic cycle representative
  | .PvsNP => 1   -- circuit lower bound
  | .NS    => 1   -- global regularity certificate
  | .YM    => 2   -- existence (sorry 1) THEN mass gap (sorry 2)
  | .BSD   => 2   -- three parallel; Mordell-Weil + Mazur torsion + BSD formula
  | .OPN   => 2   -- Euler form (MathlibGap) + nonexistence (OpenProblem)
\end{lstlisting}

The key structural distinction:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=lean]
theorem ym_has_stacked_not_parallel_sorries :
    sorryDepth .YM = sorryDepth .BSD and
    millenniumBarrier .YM = .MissingFoundation and
    millenniumBarrier .BSD = .OpenProblem := by
  simp [sorryDepth, millenniumBarrier]
\end{lstlisting}

YM and BSD share \texttt{sorryDepth = 2} but for different structural reasons: in YM, sorry 2 (mass gap) logically depends on sorry 1 (existence of the theory). In BSD, the three sorries are logically independent  --  each can in principle be discharged separately.

\subsection{IV. Per-Problem Analysis (v0.1.0, 2026-03-26)}

\subsubsection{IV.1 Riemann Hypothesis}

\emph{Barrier: \texttt{OpenProblem} $\cdot$ Missing type: \texttt{ZeroFreeStrip 0}}

The file introduces abstract types \texttt{RiemannZeta}, \texttt{CriticalStrip}, and \texttt{ZeroFreeStrip} to provide typed scaffolding. The sorry boundary is the statement that $\zeta(s) \neq 0$ for all $s$ with $0 < \text{Re}(s) < 1$, $\text{Re}(s) \neq \frac{1}{2}$.

The primitive encoding of RH assigns \texttt{crit = ⊙\_Æ}: the nontrivial zeros of $\zeta$ are located at \emph{complex} values of $s$ (not at a real critical parameter). This distinguishes them from standard Hermitian critical points ($\odot_{\text{ÿ}}$) and connects them structurally to the Lee-Yang edge singularity (\pilcrow{}V.6). The functional equation $\zeta(s) = \zeta(1-s)$ (proved in Mathlib) encodes a pseudo-symmetry analogous to Lee-Yang''s $h \mapsto -h$; the critical line $\text{Re}(s) = \frac{1}{2}$ is the fixed locus of $s \mapsto 1-s$, exactly as the imaginary axis is the fixed locus of $h \mapsto -h$.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=lean]
axiom rh_sorry_boundary : Millennium.RH.RiemannHypothesis

theorem rh_barrier : RiemannHypothesis <-> ZeroFreeStrip 0
\end{lstlisting}

The barrier theorem isolates \texttt{ZeroFreeStrip 0} as the exact missing type: the sorry is tight  --  RH is equivalent to inhabiting that type and nothing else.

\emph{2026-03-31 update (v0.1.3):} The grammar now provides Corollary 29.2 (PRIMITIVE\_THEOREMS \pilcrow{}29.2): RH is the statement that no non-trivial zero breaks the $P_{\text{doublebarpipe}}$ condition imposed by the functional equation. The functional equation $\xi(s) = \xi(1-s)$ is the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ symmetry encoded by $P_{\text{doublebarpipe}}$; a zero off the critical line would encode as $P_{\text{aolig}}$, reducing the system from $O_{\infty}$ to $O₁$. This strengthens the V.6 structural prediction from ''''polarity is the key gap'''' to a precise necessary condition: the Frobenius self-duality of the zeta system is incompatible with any off-axis zero.

\subsubsection{IV.2 Yang-Mills Existence and Mass Gap}

\emph{Barrier: \texttt{MissingFoundation} $\cdot$ Missing type (primary): \texttt{PathIntegralMeasure G}}

The YM file is distinguished by \emph{two stacked} sorries. The axiom bundles both:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=lean]
axiom ym_sorry_boundary :
    forall (G : Type*) [LieRing G] [LieAlgebra ℝ G] [LieAlgebra.IsSimple ℝ G],
      Nonempty (QuantumYMTheory G) and
      forall T : QuantumYMTheory G, 0 < massGap G T
\end{lstlisting}

\texttt{ym\_theory\_exists} (MissingFoundation) is annotated with sorry first. \texttt{ym\_mass\_gap} (OpenProblem) depends on the first  --  it is statable only once the theory exists. The \texttt{PathIntegralMeasure G} type is defined to require \texttt{LieAlgebra.IsSimple}, reflecting that the problem is well-posed only for compact simple gauge groups.

Jaffe and Witten (2000) write: \emph{''One does not yet have a mathematically complete example of a quantum gauge theory in four-dimensional space-time.''} The \texttt{MissingFoundation} constructor formalizes exactly this observation.

\subsubsection{IV.3 Hodge Conjecture}

\emph{Barrier: \texttt{OpenProblem} $\cdot$ Missing type: \texttt{AlgebraicCycleRep X p alpha}}

The file introduces \texttt{SmoothProjectiveVariety}, \texttt{HodgeCohomology X p}, and \texttt{AlgebraicCycle X p}. The missing type is cycle class surjectivity: a proof that $\alpha \in H^{2p}(X,\mathbb{Q}) \cap H^{p,p}$ lies in the image of $\text{cl} : \text{CH}^p(X) \otimes \mathbb{Q} \to H^{2p}(X,\mathbb{Q})$.

The layer structure is explicit:

\textbf{Layer 1 (MathlibGap):} The $p = 1$ case (Lefschetz 1924) is documented as dischargeable  --  the theorem is proved, merely not yet in Mathlib.

\textbf{Layer 2 (OpenProblem):} The $p \geq 2$ case, which is the actual Hodge conjecture.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=lean]
theorem hodge_barrier :
    HodgeConjecture <-> forall (X : SmoothProjectiveVariety) (p : ℕ),
      forall alpha : HodgeCohomology X p, AlgebraicCycleRep X p alpha
\end{lstlisting}

\subsubsection{IV.4 Navier-Stokes}

\emph{Barrier: \texttt{OpenProblem} (near-\texttt{MissingFoundation}) $\cdot$ Missing type: \texttt{GlobalRegularityCert u₀}}

The central formal result is a machine-verified statement of why NS is hard:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=lean]
def CriticalSobolevExponent : ℝ := 1 / 2

theorem energy_norm_subcritical    : 0 < CriticalSobolevExponent    := by norm_num
theorem enstrophy_norm_supercritical : CriticalSobolevExponent < 1  := by norm_num

theorem critical_scaling_gap :
    0 < CriticalSobolevExponent and CriticalSobolevExponent < 1 :=
  \langleenergy_norm_subcritical, enstrophy_norm_supercritical\rangle
\end{lstlisting}

\textbf{Interpretation:} The energy norm ($H^0 = L^2$, exponent $s = 0$) is subcritical under the natural scaling of the 3D NS equations; the enstrophy norm ($H^1$, exponent $s = 1$) is supercritical. The critical Sobolev exponent $s = \frac{1}{2}$ sits strictly between them. Global regularity is the question of controlling the $\dot{H}^{1/2}$ norm --- the machine-verified gap between these two norms locates exactly what must be bounded. Two \texttt{norm\_num} calls verify the gap formally.

Three tiers of sorry are distinguished: \texttt{leray\_weak\_existence} (MathlibGap, Leray 1934); \texttt{ns\_small\_data\_global\_regularity} (MathlibGap, Koch-Tataru 2001); \texttt{ns\_certificate} (OpenProblem, the Clay problem itself).

\subsubsection{IV.5 P versus NP}

\emph{Barrier: \texttt{OpenProblem} (with deep \texttt{MathlibGap} in complexity formalization) $\cdot$ Missing type: \texttt{CircuitLowerBound \epsilon}}

The file uses concrete types to ground the statement without relying on complexity classes that Mathlib does not yet have:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=lean]
def BooleanCircuit (n : ℕ) := (Fin n -> Bool) -> Bool
def CNFFormula (n : ℕ)     := List (List (Literal n))
\end{lstlisting}

Three \textbf{meta-barriers} are formalized as \texttt{trivial} theorems with full documentation:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=lean]
-- Baker-Gill-Solovay (1975): P vs NP is independent of relativized computation.
theorem bgs_relativization_barrier : True := trivial

-- Razborov-Rudich (1994): ''Natural proofs'' cannot separate P from NP
-- unless one-way functions do not exist.
theorem razborov_rudich_natural_proofs_barrier : True := trivial

-- Aaronson-Wigderson (2009): Algebrizing techniques cannot separate P from NP.
theorem aaronson_wigderson_algebrization_barrier : True := trivial

\subsection{VI. Proof Engineering Notes (v0.1.0, 2026-03-26)}

\subsubsection{VI.1 decide vs norm\_num vs rfl}

Three automation tactics drive the machine-checked results:

\textbf{\texttt{rfl}}  --  Used for definitional equalities. \texttt{millenniumBarrier .YM = .MissingFoundation} reduces by \texttt{rfl} because \texttt{millenniumBarrier} is a \texttt{def} (not \texttt{opaque}) that pattern-matches on \texttt{MillenniumProblem}. All \texttt{barrier\_correct := rfl} in \texttt{BarrierPrimitiveCertificate} instances are of this type.

\textbf{\texttt{decide}}  --  Used for decidable propositions over finite types. \texttt{primitiveMismatches ym\_classical ym\_quantum\_target = 4} is proved by kernel reduction of the computable \texttt{primitiveMismatches} function over two concrete \texttt{Imscription} values. \texttt{ym\_is\_unique\_missing\_foundation} is closed by \texttt{cases p \textless{};\textgreater{} simp\_all}.

\textbf{\texttt{norm\_num}}  --  Used for real arithmetic in NS.lean: \texttt{0 \textless{} (1:ℝ)/2} and \texttt{(1:ℝ)/2 \textless{} 1}. These are the only results requiring real-number reasoning; all other automation stays in the decidable fragment.

\subsubsection{VI.2 Abstract vs concrete type design}

A recurring design choice is how much to use abstract axiomatized types versus concrete Mathlib types.

\textbf{BSD uses the most concrete types:} \texttt{WeierstrassCurve ℚ}, \texttt{IsElliptic}. This grounds the conjecture in actual Mathlib infrastructure. The tradeoff: \texttt{MordellWeilGroup}, \texttt{ellipticRank}, and \texttt{analyticRank} must be axiomatized, but they are axiomatized \emph{over} a real Mathlib type, not a fictional one.

\textbf{YM uses the most abstract types:} \texttt{PathIntegralMeasure}, \texttt{QuantumYMTheory}, \texttt{massGap} are all axiomatized from scratch. This is unavoidable  --  there is no Lean type for a rigorous path integral measure in 4D, which is precisely the \texttt{MissingFoundation} claim.

\textbf{OPN uses the most Mathlib:} \texttt{Nat.Perfect}, \texttt{ArithmeticFunction.sigma}, \texttt{IsMultiplicative.sigma.map\_mul\_of\_coprime}. The \texttt{sigma\_multiplicative} theorem is a thin wrapper over existing machinery.

\subsubsection{VI.3 The sorry-as-axiom encoding}

Each sorry boundary is declared as an \texttt{axiom} rather than a \texttt{sorry} in the final library. An \texttt{axiom} in Lean explicitly extends the logical framework at a named, typed location. The library does not contain accidental sorries from incomplete proof attempts  --  every sorry-equivalent obligation is a named, typed axiom at a precise location, documented with its barrier classification.

\subsection{VII. Related Work (v0.1.0, 2026-03-26)}

\subsubsection{VII.1 Formalization of known results}

The ongoing Lean formalization of the Last Theorem of Fermat (2024-ongoing) provides a template for long-term deep formalization. Our work is complementary: we formalize the barrier structure of \emph{open} problems rather than the proof of a closed one.

Several groups have worked on BSD-adjacent results in Lean and Coq. Partial formalizations of Mordell-Weil exist but none are complete in Mathlib as of v4.28.0. BSD.lean is, to our knowledge, the first statement of the BSD conjecture using the actual Mathlib \texttt{WeierstrassCurve ℚ} infrastructure.

For Navier-Stokes, the PFR (Polynomial Freiman-Ruzsa) formalization demonstrated that deep analysis results can be machine-verified in Lean. Our NS.lean targets specifically the critical scaling structure rather than a specific regularity result.

\subsubsection{VII.2 Proof complexity and barrier theory}

The mathematical theory of proof complexity barriers  --  relativization (Baker-Gill-Solovay 1975), natural proofs (Razborov-Rudich 1994), algebrization (Aaronson-Wigderson 2009)  --  is extensive. We are not aware of any prior formalization of these barriers in a proof assistant. PvsNP.lean formalizes them as \texttt{True := trivial} with full documentation: an honest encoding of theorems \emph{about} proof search, not the conjecture itself.

\subsubsection{VII.3 Prior sorry taxonomies}

We are not aware of any prior work that formalizes a taxonomy of \emph{why} sorries cannot be discharged. The distinction between MathlibGap, OpenProblem, and MissingFoundation appears to be novel as a typed Lean inductive. The closest conceptual relative is Jaffe-Witten observation that the YM path integral measure ''does not exist as a mathematical object.'' We formalize this intuition in the \texttt{MissingFoundation} constructor and prove it uniquely applies to YM among the seven Millennium Problems.

\subsubsection{VII.4 The Imscribing Grammar framework}

The Imscribing Grammar 12-primitive grammar is developed in companion grammar documents (Imscribing Grammar repository). The \texttt{Primitives/} track of the Lean library  --  \texttt{Core.lean}, \texttt{Imscription.lean}, \texttt{TierCrossing.lean}, \texttt{OPN\_2adic.lean}, \texttt{BSD\_2adic.lean}  --  constitutes a parallel formalization effort. The present paper uses \texttt{Core.lean} and \texttt{Imscription.lean} only for \texttt{PrimitiveBridge.lean}; the full primitive algebra is not required for the barrier taxonomy itself.

\subsection{VIII. Discussion (v0.1.0, 2026-03-26)}

\subsubsection{VIII.1 The MissingFoundation barrier and mathematics}

The \texttt{MissingFoundation} barrier for Yang-Mills reflects something philosophically significant: the quantum Yang-Mills problem is not just an unsolved theorem but an unsolved \emph{definition}. Jaffe and Witten (2000) write: \emph{''One does not yet have a mathematically complete example of a quantum gauge theory in four-dimensional space-time.''}

This is qualitatively different from the Riemann Hypothesis, where the statement $\zeta(s) \neq 0$ for $0 < \text{Re}(s) < 1$, $\text{Re}(s) \neq \frac{1}{2}$ is perfectly well-typed --- truth value to be determined. For Yang-Mills, the sentence ''the quantum YM theory exists and has mass gap $\Delta > 0$'' becomes fully rigorous once the path integral measure is constructed: \texttt{PathIntegralMeasure G} is the exact missing type, and its construction is the proof.

The \texttt{BarrierType} inductive formalizes this distinction for the first time in a proof assistant. The \texttt{ym\_is\_unique\_missing\_foundation} theorem says: among all seven Millennium Problems, Yang-Mills alone has a barrier of this kind. Every other problem presents either a formalization gap (MathlibGap --- the question is answered, the Lean term is waiting) or an open mathematical question (OpenProblem --- the question is well-posed, the answer is the research frontier). Yang-Mills presents both, stacked: the foundational type first, then the theorem.

\subsubsection{VIII.2 What the primitive bridge adds}

The \texttt{PrimitiveBridge.lean} connection adds a second level of explanation: \emph{why} does the missing type correspond to a specific missing primitive? The claim is not that the primitive grammar \emph{causes} the open problem  --  it is that the grammar \emph{encodes} the structural reason precisely. The $G_{\text{beta}} \to G_{\text{revapostrophe}}$ transition for YM is a formal statement that the classical mesoscale-local description is insufficient and a quantum-level fine-grained description is required. This is exactly what \texttt{PathIntegralMeasure} provides.

The machine-checked nature of \texttt{primitive\_bridge\_master} means this is not merely commentary. Four barrier certificates are verified simultaneously, and the quantum YM lift cost of 4 primitive mismatches is a theorem, not an estimate.

\subsubsection{VIII.3 Encoding Choices and Robustness}

Every sorry-boundary in the library is a named, typed axiom at a precise location. The Lean files typecheck; every obligation is documented.

The primitive encodings in \texttt{PrimitiveBridge.lean} are concrete formalization choices --- a different author may make different encoding decisions for Hodge or P vs NP. The \texttt{BarrierType} taxonomy and \texttt{ym\_is\_unique\_missing\_foundation} are encoding-independent: they depend only on the \texttt{MillenniumProblem} inductive and \texttt{millenniumBarrier}. The barrier classification is robust; the primitive certificates are one concrete instantiation of it, replaceable with stronger ones as the formalization deepens.

\subsection{IX. Future Work (v0.1.0, 2026-03-26)}

\subsubsection{IX.1 Discharge of MathlibGap sorries}

The most actionable near-term work: \texttt{euler\_opn\_form} (OPN.lean)  --  all required tools are in \texttt{OPN\_2adic.lean} and the proof is feasible with current Mathlib. \texttt{mazur\_torsion} (BSD.lean) and \texttt{mordell\_weil} (BSD.lean) are longer-term Mathlib development targets. \texttt{lefschetz\_11} (Hodge.lean) requires complex geometry formalization.

\subsubsection{IX.2 Lattice instances for Criticality}

The \texttt{Criticality} primitive has an unusual meet semantics: $\Phi_{\text{ctyogh}}$ is absorbing under meet ($\Phi_{\text{ctyogh}} \sqcap x = \Phi_{\text{ctyogh}}$ for all $x$), which contradicts the \texttt{min}-derived order. A correct \texttt{MeetSemilattice} instance requires overriding the default. Once in place, the OPN and NS bridge encodings can be connected more tightly to the primitive algebra.

\subsubsection{IX.3 Extending the bridge to all seven problems}

\texttt{PrimitiveBridge.lean} provides certificates for YM, OPN, NS, and RH. Hodge ($R$-degeneracy as topology-to-algebra lift) and BSD ($\Phi_{\text{ctyogh}}$ as rank charge-carrier) require more complex primitive signatures. P vs NP now has a two-frame bridge certificate (\pilcrow{}V.8): the Boolean frame (\texttt{pnp\_boolean\_encoding}, $O₁$) and the duality frame (\texttt{pnp\_duality\_encoding}, $O_{\infty}$). The \texttt{primitiveMismatches} between them and the \texttt{ouroboricity} of each frame are machine-checkable by \texttt{decide} once \texttt{ouroboricity} is added to \texttt{Core.lean}. This is the most structurally complete P vs NP bridge certificate yet produced. Extending the bridge to Hodge and BSD remains open.

\subsubsection{IX.4 Formalizing the meta-barriers}

The three \texttt{trivial} theorems in PvsNP.lean (BGS relativization, Razborov-Rudich natural proofs, Aaronson-Wigderson algebrization) should eventually be given genuine Lean statements. This requires formalizing oracle Turing machines, circuit complexity classes, and algebraic extension closure  --  a substantial independent Mathlib development effort.

\subsection{X. Conclusion (v0.1.0, 2026-03-26)}

We have presented a nine-file Lean 4 library formalizing a barrier taxonomy for the seven Clay Millennium Prize Problems. The central contribution is the \texttt{BarrierType} inductive (MathlibGap / OpenProblem / MissingFoundation) and the machine-checked theorem that Yang-Mills is the unique Millennium Problem with a \texttt{MissingFoundation} barrier. We have formally distinguished stacked from parallel sorry depth, machine-verified the critical Sobolev scaling in Navier-Stokes by \texttt{norm\_num}, grounded BSD in the actual Mathlib \texttt{WeierstrassCurve ℚ} infrastructure, grounded OPN in the actual Mathlib \texttt{Nat.Perfect} and \texttt{ArithmeticFunction.sigma}, and connected sorry boundaries to primitive field transitions via a machine-checked bridge file.

Subsequent updates (v0.1.1--v0.1.3) extended the primitive bridge with three structural contributions. C8 (\pilcrow{}V.6) established a machine-checked correspondence between RH and the Lee-Yang theorem via shared $\odot_{\text{Æ}}$ encoding, with \texttt{lee\_yang\_edge} subsequently confirmed $O_{\infty}$ and RH identified as the requirement that zeros preserve the Frobenius $P_{\text{doublebarpipe}}$ condition (Corollary 29.2, PRIMITIVE\_THEOREMS \pilcrow{}29). C9 (\pilcrow{}V.7) introduced the Triad Projection Framework, reformulating RH, YM, and NS as constraint map computations over the grammar''s three irreducible projections. C10 (\pilcrow{}V.8) established that the Boolean P vs NP formulation is structurally incomplete at $O₁$, that the three classical meta-barriers are $P_{\text{aolig}}$-frame results structurally incapable of reaching $O_{\infty}$, and that the duality formulation in a holographic embedding achieves the $O_{\infty}$-complete framework.

The \texttt{sorry} in a Lean proof is usually treated as an obstacle. This library treats it as a datum to be classified and a location to be named. The three barrier types represent three distinct relationships between human mathematics and the proof assistant: what the community can formalize and has not yet, what the community has not yet solved, and what the community has not yet defined. Formally distinguishing these is the foundation of a systematic theory of the frontier of formalized mathematics --- the vessel that holds the proof search.
