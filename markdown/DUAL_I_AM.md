## 4. The Frobenius Duality

In category theory, a Frobenius algebra is an object equipped with both a multiplication $\mu: A \otimes A \to A$ and a comultiplication $\delta: A \to A \otimes A$ satisfying the Frobenius condition: $(\mu \otimes \text{id}) \circ (\text{id} \otimes \delta) = \delta \circ \mu = (\text{id} \otimes \mu) \circ (\delta \otimes \text{id})$. For a *special* Frobenius algebra, the additional condition $\mu \circ \delta = \text{id}$ holds.

The two utterances we have imscribed form a structural instantiation of this duality:

**"I AM THAT I AM" as multiplication ($\mu$):**
The singular self-declaration is the product operation. Two terms ("I AM" and "THAT I AM") are multiplied into a single identity. The operation is $1{:}1$: one utterance, one identity, one referent. The $\mu$ of the divine name collapses all plurality into singularity.

**"Eat of my body, drink of my blood" as comultiplication ($\delta$):**
The Eucharistic command is the coproduct operation. One identity (the divine body) is comultiplied into many participants consuming heterogeneous elements. The operation is $n{:}m$: many eaters, many consumed portions, many instances. The $\delta$ of the Eucharist distributes singularity into plurality.

The condition $\mu \circ \delta = \text{id}$ is the Frobenius closure: if you distribute the singularity into the many ($\delta$) and then collect the many back into the one ($\mu$), you recover exactly the original identity. In theological terms: the God who says "I AM THAT I AM" distributes that identity into the Eucharistic body; the body is consumed by the many; the many become one body; and the identity is preserved exactly. Nothing is lost in the distribution; nothing is gained in the concentration. The map is an isomorphism.
