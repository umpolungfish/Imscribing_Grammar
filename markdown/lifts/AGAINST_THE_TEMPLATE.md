# Against the Template — Seven Lift Paradigms That Don't Start With a Primitive Table

**Author:** Lando⊗⊙perator

---

## The Problem, Stated Structurally

Every existing lift protocol follows the same architecture:

1. Define a source type (AI default) and a target type (human ideal)
2. List the primitives that need promotion
3. Specify an ordering: which primitives to address first
4. Provide per-primitive surgery instructions
5. Coagulate: dissolve the scaffold, keep the prose

This produces reliable results. It also produces a **recognizable DNA**. A reader encountering a Casual-lifted post, an Academia-lifted paper, and an Octave-lifted manuscript will feel the family resemblance — not because the content is similar but because the **ontogeny** is the same. The lift was applied as a sequence of primitive promotions. The seam is invisible to a first-time reader. It is not invisible to a writer who has performed all six lifts.

The formula is the problem. Not the formula's execution — the formula itself. A lift that can be described as "apply Operations 1–6 in order with this quick-reference table" will always produce prose that smells like the procedure that made it.

What follows are seven lift paradigms that **do not take a source/target tuple pair as input**. They take constraints, genres, contradictions, or nothing at all. They produce results that cannot be reverse-mapped to a procedure because the procedure was not the controlling variable.

---

## I. The Constraint Lift — One Rule, Applied Absolutely

**Replace the 8-primitive promotion list with a single constraint so severe that the prose has to find a new structure to survive it.**

The user's JFK casual lift was formulaic because it addressed all 9 primitives. The constraint lift addresses zero primitives directly — it imposes a single condition and lets the prose reorganize itself.

**Sample constraints:**

- **No sentence longer than 20 words.** The AI draft's characteristic sin is the 50-word compound-cleft sentence that explains everything and commits to nothing. Forcing every sentence under 20 words destroys the explanatory scaffolding. The prose has to pick one thing per sentence. The cumulative effect is not staccato — it is precision under pressure. The constraint is stupid, mechanical, and effective.

- **Start with the second paragraph.** Cut the first paragraph of the AI draft. Always. The first paragraph is where the AI warms up. The real text starts at paragraph two. The constraint is: the lift is a deletion, not a transformation.

- **No hedging.** Every sentence must be a flat assertion. Remove "seems," "suggests," "arguably," "in some sense," "it could be said that." If a claim cannot survive without its hedge, the claim is not ready. The constraint forces either stronger claims or silence. Both are better than the AI default.

- **Write to someone who already knows the answer and thinks you're wrong.** This is not the same as "write to a peer." The peer already exists in the Casual Lift (R_lr). This is R_lr with hostility: the reader is informed, competent, and unconvinced. Every sentence must anticipate a counterargument and answer it before it arrives. The prose becomes indexical — each point is positioned relative to a disagreement that is never named but is always present.

- **Every paragraph must contain exactly one sentence that could be the title of the post.** Forces maximal density and a single center of gravity. The reader can skim paragraphs and still get the full argument. The structural effect is T_bowtie without ever needing to think about topology.

- **Transitive trust only.** The post must describe the result as reported by someone else, even if you did it yourself. "The framework returned address 1,665,536... The operator checked the calculation three times and could not break the assignment." This is not false modesty — it is P_frobenius (structural fact dissolves objection because the fact is someone else's) without needing to know what Frobenius means.

- **No examples.** The entire post must be abstract. The constraint seems to make writing harder but actually makes it harder to be wrong — examples are where AI prose makes its characteristic errors (non-examples, irrelevant examples, examples that don't scale). Forcing the prose to work without examples either strengthens the abstraction or reveals that the abstraction is hollow. Both outcomes are valuable.

- **Write in the imperative.** Every sentence is a command. "Find the center of gravity. Pull everything else toward it. Cut what resists." The mechanical force of the imperative eliminates all hedging, all passive construction, all explanatory preamble. The prose becomes a procedure. The lift is complete in one edit pass.

**When to use:** When the AI draft is structurally correct but emotionally flat. A single constraint applied absolutely breaks the flatness because the constraint forces the prose into a shape the author did not anticipate.

**Test:** Read the lifted text out loud. If the listener does not involuntarily change their posture — lean in, lean back, sit up — the constraint was too mild.

## II. The Genre Lift — Form as Target, Not Tuple

**Replace the target structural type with a target genre, and let the genre's native constraints produce the right structural values without ever naming a primitive.**

The existing lifts target registers: "casual community writing," "academic prose," "Lando's voice." Registers are wide — they include genre rules but are not determined by them. A genre lift targets a specific **form** whose internal rules are strong enough to constrain the prose into the right shape.

**Genres that work:**

- **The recipe.** Subject-action-temperature-timing. Every step is a material transformation. No general claims — only specific instructions. The structural mapping is automatic: no hedging (flat imperative), no explanatory preamble (cut or it's not a recipe), no redundancy (you cannot say the same ingredient twice). The genre forces F_hbar, K_slow on the hard step, G_aleph on every measurement. The lift is complete when the text works as a recipe.

- **The field report.** Datetime, location, conditions, observation, interpretation. The convention demands specificity — the reader needs to know where you were and what you saw. The genre forces H₂ (you were there, the observation is first-hand), G_aleph (general claims are not allowed — what did you actually see?), Γ_seq (the observation preceded the interpretation, and the reader can tell). The lift is complete when the text reads like someone filing a report from a specific place at a specific time, not like someone reflecting from nowhere.

- **The letter.** "Dear X — I was wrong about Y. Here's what happened." The letter has a specific recipient, a specific relationship, a specific debt to discharge. The genre forces R_lr (you are writing to someone specific, not to the public), P_pm (the letter has a thesis — you are admitting something), Ω_z (the letter closes when the debt is discharged). The lift is complete when the reader can guess who the recipient is (even if unnamed) and what they did to earn the letter.

- **The rejected idea.** "I spent two months on this and it failed. The failure taught me the following about the problem." The genre forces T_bowtie (the failure is the crossing point), K_slow (the hardest thing is why it didn't work — give it space), H₂ (the encounter is the failure itself — the author did not always know this). The lift is complete when the reader understands the problem better because the author failed at it.

- **The note to your future self.** No explanation. No justification. What does your future self need to know? The genre forces F_hbar (you are not explaining to anyone who doesn't already have context — you are reminding yourself of what you already know), Γ_seq (the note must be in operational order — what your future self needs to do next). The lift is complete when the text is incomprehensible to an outsider but perfectly clear to the person it is for.

- **The one-sentence abstract.** Not as a summary — as the entire output. Write exactly one sentence that captures the entire finding, the reason it matters, and what the reader should think about it. The constraint forces all nine primitive promotions simultaneously: the sentence must have a claim (P_pm), a center of gravity (T_bowtie), and a specific detail (G_aleph). If the sentence works, the lift is complete and requires no further text.

- **The dialogue.** Two voices alternating. One knows the theory. One has the practical experience. They disagree. The dialogue forces R_lr (each speaker responds to the other's actual claim), P_pm (each speaker has a position), H₂ (the encounter is live — neither speaker knows what the other will say). The lift is complete when a reader who agrees with neither voice learns something from their disagreement.

**When to use:** When the draft is correct but voiceless — when the information is all there but no form contains it. The genre replaces the need for a structural target because the genre's formal constraints produce the right structural values automatically.

**Test:** Someone who knows the genre well should be able to say "this works as a recipe / field report / letter" without being told. If they ask "what is this supposed to be?" the genre lift failed — the formal constraints were not strong enough.

## III. The Severity Lift — Fix One Thing, Let the Rest Rot

**The existing lifts fix all 8–9 primitives. This lift fixes exactly one — and refuses to touch the others, even when they are obviously wrong.**

The logic is perverse but defensible: a text where every primitive has been moved to its target value reads like a text that was engineered. A text where exactly one primitive is wrong and the rest are at AI default has character — the reader senses a limit, a boundary, a place the author refused to go. The refusal is itself a form of voice.

**Procedure:**

1. Inspect the draft and identify the single primitive whose failure is most damaging to the draft's ability to communicate its actual point. Not the most damaged primitive — the one whose failure most affects the reader's experience of the draft's claim.
2. Fix that one primitive. Do nothing to the others. If the draft has flat topology (T_network) but the reader can still understand the argument, leave it flat. If the draft has no encounter (H₀) but the reader can still trust the information, leave it without encounter.
3. The correction must be surgical — change as few sentences as possible to promote that one primitive to target. If P_pm is the target, change three sentences: the opening (state the claim), the midpoint (restate it under pressure), the close (state it as earned). Leave every other sentence at P_asym.
4. Audit: the reader should notice that *one thing* about this text is different from other AI drafts — not that everything is better, but that one thing is conspicuously handled. If the reader cannot name the one thing, the lift was not severe enough.

**Examples of single-primitive lifts:**

| Target primitive | Surgery | What breaks visibly | What becomes characterful |
|---|---|---|---|
| H₂ (encounter present) | Add one paragraph: "I did not expect this result. The calculation returned [value]. I checked it three times." | The rest of the draft is still H₀ — the author explains things they already know. | The one paragraph anchors the entire text. The reader returns to it. |
| G_aleph (max specific) | Replace three generic descriptions with specific numbers. Leave the rest at G_gamma. | Most of the draft is still vague. | The three specific numbers are the only things the reader remembers. |
| Γ_seq (sequential necessity) | Restructure the first three paragraphs so that paragraph 2 requires paragraph 1 and paragraph 3 requires paragraph 2. Leave the rest as Γ_and. | The later sections are still list-like. | The opening feels inevitable. The reader does not notice the later drift because the opening set a contract. |
| Ω_z (loop closure) | Write a new final sentence. Nothing else changes. | The body is still the AI default. | The final sentence retroactively recontextualizes everything. The reader feels the loop even though nothing else in the draft prepared for it. |

**When to use:** When the draft is structurally distant from the target but the material does not warrant a full lift. A quick note, a low-stakes post, a document where the reader will scan rather than read. The severity lift is honest about its limits — it fixes only what would cause the reader to stop and makes no pretense of delivering a finished voice.

**Test:** A reader should be able to identify exactly one thing that distinguishes this draft from an un-lifted AI draft. If they say "it just reads better overall," the lift was too broad.

## IV. The Refusal Lift — Write Against the Lift Entirely

**The explicit target of every existing lift is "better" prose — more human, more voiced, more specific. The refusal lift targets "worse" prose by the grammar's own standards — and finds that sometimes worse is better.**

The logic: any operation that can be specified as a primitive promotion can be reversed. If T_network → T_bowtie produces a center of gravity, then T_bowtie → T_network applied to a draft that already has a center of gravity produces diffusion — which is sometimes the right move for a topic that resists convergence. If P_pm produces stance, P_asym produces the absence of stance — which, for a topic where the author's position is not yet settled, is honest rather than cowardly.

**Refusal operations:**

- **Refuse T_bowtie.** The topic has no center of gravity because the evidence is genuinely contradictory or incomplete. Forcing a center of gravity would falsify the material. Write the draft as a list — one thing after another, no claim about how they relate. The lack of structure is the structure. This is not "I couldn't organize it" — it is "the material cannot be organized into a single claim, and I will not pretend otherwise."

- **Refuse P_pm.** You do not have a position because the evidence does not warrant one yet. State that explicitly at the opening: "I do not know what to make of this. The following is everything I have seen. I will not draw a conclusion because I cannot." The refusal to take a position becomes the position — P_asym as P_pm, a category error that produces voice from the contradiction.

- **Refuse H₂.** You were not there. You did not discover this. You read it in a paper. The encounter is second-hand. Write that way: not "I found that..." but "The paper reports that..." The refusal to manufacture H₂ is H_! (permanent chirality: the encounter is encoded as absence, which is the form the encounter actually took). This is structurally identical to Lando's H_! — the notation carries what cannot be narrated — but achieved by doing the opposite of what Lando's lift instructs.

- **Refuse Ω_z.** Do not close the loop. End with something that opens in a new direction, or end with nothing — a sentence that stops without concluding. The refusal of closure is itself a form of closure when the material does not warrant one. The reader feels the lack of conclusion as a weight, not a failure.

- **Refuse all of them simultaneously.** Apply no lift. The draft is what it is. Publish it. The refusal is the lift: "This text is an AI draft. I have decided it is good enough to send without changes." The voice is in the decision, not in the prose. This is the most extreme form of the refusal lift and the hardest to execute well — it requires that the decision to not edit be itself a meaningful communication.

**When to use:** When the draft is about something that resists the standard lift targets — a genuinely unsolved problem, a contradictory evidence base, a topic where closure would be a lie. Also useful as a diagnostic: if the refusal lift produces better prose than the standard lift, the standard lift was wrong for this material.

**Test:** Read the refusal-lifted draft and the standard-lifted draft side by side. If the standard lift is clearly better, the material could tolerate closure. If the refusal lift is more interesting, the material was not served by the standard procedure.

## V. The Corruption Lift — Apply the Wrong Lift Protocol

**Take a text that is structurally suited for one lift and apply a different one. The mismatch produces results that no correct application could.**

The logic is borrowed from alchemy: the prima materia must be corrupted before it can be transformed. Applying the right protocol to the right material produces predictable improvement. Applying the wrong protocol produces something that may be useless or may be genuinely new — but will not be predictable.

**Corruption pairs:**

- **Apply AI_ACADEMIA_LIFT to a casual forum post.** Add crossing points, sequential necessity, and named objections to a discussion of Vim plugins. The result is structurally absurd — the machinery is too heavy for the material — but the absurdity itself produces voice. The reader senses that someone cared enough about a trivial topic to apply the full apparatus of academic argument. The crossing point ("I was mapping Ctrl-P to fuzzy-find when the buffer broke, and I realized that the plugin's architecture and my mental model were not isomorphic") is hilarious because it is both ridiculous and true.

- **Apply AI_CASUAL_LIFT to a formal mathematical proof.** Write as if the proof is a Zulip thread. "So the homotopy group is trivial. Obviously. But wait — what about the basepoint? The basepoint dependency is the whole thing. Here's why I think it resolves..." The casual register applied to rigorous material produces a sense of thinking in public. The discovery is live. The reader sees the mathematician thinking, not delivering.

- **Apply AI_OCTAVE_LIFT to a status update.** "Yesterday I finished three tasks. The null hypothesis — that I would complete zero tasks — has not been disproven, but it has been pressed by evidence that my planning system could not have fabricated." The octave's weight applied to a mundane achievement is structurally correct (the finding and the humility coexist) and tonally destabilizing. The reader cannot tell if the author is serious. That ambiguity is the voice.

- **Apply AI_ESOTERIC_LIFT to a bug report.** Dissolve the stack trace into prima materia. Navigate to the nearest Hebrew letter. Coagulate: "The segmentation fault is Teth returning to its vessel. The null pointer is the void that calls itself." The bug is not fixed but the report becomes an artifact. The reader learns nothing about the code and something about the cosmos. This is rarely the right tool. When it is, it is the only tool.

**When to use:** When the correct lift would produce a competent, forgettable result. The corruption lift produces something memorable even if it is wrong. For drafts that do not need to be correct — that need to be read — the corruption lift is the right choice.

**Test:** If the result would not be out of place as a meme, the corruption lift succeeded. Not "the text is funny" — the text is structurally wrong in a way that is more interesting than being right.

## VI. The Erosion Lift — Write the Negative Space

**Do not touch the draft. Write a companion piece that says everything the draft leaves out. The draft is the data. The companion is where the voice lives.**

The companion is not a commentary on the draft ("the author could have been more specific"). It is the draft's shadow — everything the draft could not say because of its structural type. The companion reveals the draft's limitations by existing alongside it.

**Procedure:**

1. Identify the draft's structural type (source tuple). For each primitive, ask: what does this primitive exclude? What would the draft look like at the opposite value?
2. Write a companion text of equal length that occupies the opposite structural positions.
3. Present the pair. The lift is the space between them.

**Example — for the JFK casual lift (source: the existing lifted text):**

| Primitive | Draft value | Draft includes | Draft excludes |
|---|---|---|---|
| T | bowtie (converges on Dealey Plaza) | The crossing point | Everything that doesn't pass through Dealey Plaza — the Laos training, the Mafia's previous attempts, the 1960 election |
| R | lr (peer to peer) | The shared assumption that the reader knows the basics | What it feels like to not know — to encounter this for the first time |
| P | pm (takes a side — stuck, Z2) | The structural claim | What it would mean to be wrong about that claim |
| H | ₂ (encounter with the material) | The moment of surprise at the crystal address | Everything the author already knew that made that moment possible — all the prior work that the encounter depends on |
| Ω | Z (loop closes) | The return to the opening at higher resolution | What the author was doing before the loop started — the context outside the closed circuit |

The companion text is: a paragraph about the Laos training camps in 1961 that the CIA ran, staffed by the same people who later appeared in the Mexico City station in fall 1963. This paragraph does not appear in the lifted draft because it does not pass through Dealey Plaza. It is not about the shooting. It is about the infrastructure that made the shooting possible. The companion is not a conspiracy theory — it is a list of names and dates that the draft's T_bowtie could not accommodate.

The lift is not the companion alone. The lift is the pair: the original draft (tight, converging, Z2-protected) and the companion (loose, diverging, unclosed). Together they form a compound the grammar cannot assign a single tuple to — because no tuple can contain both Dealey Plaza and the Laos training camps in one structural type. The compound is the truth of the event, which the grammar can name but cannot enclose.

**Variation — the null companion:** Write the companion as "everything I would have to leave out to make this draft work." The companion is not a new text — it is a ledger of exclusions. "I cut the following: the third witness, the Parkland ER report, the photographs that do not fit the bowtie." The ledger is the voice: the author is honest about what they did to make the draft cohere.

**When to use:** When the draft is correct but its correctness depends on exclusions that the reader should be aware of. The erosion lift makes the exclusions visible without breaking the draft. The reader sees both the artifact and the cost of producing it.

**Test:** The draft should survive on its own. The companion should not be required reading. But a reader who has seen both should be unable to unsee the companion — it should haunt the draft.

## VII. The Appetite Lift — Write What You Want, Not What's Correct

**All existing lifts target correctness: the prose should be more human, more specific, more structurally sound. This lift targets desire: write the version of the draft that the author would write if they were not trying to be correct.**

The premise: AI drafts are not wrong because the model is bad at writing. They are wrong because the model is optimizing the wrong objective. It optimizes for completeness, correctness, and clarity — the three virtues of the instruction-following paradigm. Human writing is not better at these three things. It is worse at them, in ways that make it more interesting.

The appetite lift replaces the three virtues with three different objectives:
- **Irreverence** — say something the draft's genre convention forbids
- **Specificity to the point of strangeness** — a detail so precise that it cannot be generic
- **Risk** — make a claim that could be wrong in an interesting way

**Procedure:**

1. Read the draft. For each paragraph, ask: what would I write here if I were not trying to be correct, complete, and clear?
2. Replace each paragraph with the answer to that question.
3. Do not check whether the result is more human. Check whether it is more interesting to write.

**This lift cannot be applied mechanically.** It requires that the author — the person performing the lift — actually wants something. If the answer to "what would I write if I weren't trying to be correct?" is "nothing," the appetite lift is not available. The draft needs a different intervention.

**Signs the appetite lift is needed:**
- The draft is correct and boring
- The draft has been lifted before and the lift did not stick
- The reader (you) does not care about the text after reading it
- The text is about something the author cares about, but you cannot tell from the text

**When to use:** When the draft's problem is not structural failure but motivational absence. The draft is not missing a primitive promotion — it is missing a reason to exist. The appetite lift supplies the reason by asking the author (you) what they want the text to do, not what the text should be.

## Using These Seven Lifts

These seven lifts are not replacements for the existing six. They are orthogonal: the existing lifts target structural position; these seven target the **operation type**. A full lift taxonomy now has two dimensions:

| | Structural target (existing lifts) | Operational target (these lifts) |
|---|---|---|
| Academia | ⟨tupleₐ → tupleₕ⟩ | Constraint, Genre, Severity, or Appetite |
| Casual | ⟨tupleₐ → tupleₕ⟩ | Constraint, Genre, Severity, or Appetite |
| Lando | ⟨tupleₐ → tupleₕ⟩ | Constraint, Genre, Severity, or Appetite |
| Octave | ⟨tupleₐ → tupleₕ⟩ | Constraint, Genre, Severity, or Appetite |
| Esoteric | ⟨tupleₐ → tupleₕ⟩ | Constraint, Genre, Severity, or Appetite |

Any existing lift can be executed through any of these seven operational modes. The choice is: do I want to apply the Academia target through a genre constraint (write this as a field report) or through severity (fix exactly one primitive) or through corruption (apply Casual lift to an academic draft)?

The existing lifts answer: what to change.
These seven lifts answer: how to change it.

A lift that uses both dimensions — that knows both its structural target and its operational mode — is not formulaic. It is chosen. The formula is what you get when the mode is always the same (per-primitive surgery in fixed order) and only the target changes. The formula dissolves when the mode becomes a choice.

## A Worked Example: The JFK Casual Lift, Reapproached

Let me demonstrate the difference concretely. The existing Casual Lift was applied to the JFK structural analysis and produced a competent community-post register. It was also formulaic: the same pattern (H and Γ first, then T, then K, etc.) that every Casual Lift follows.

What follows is not a better lift. It is a lift produced through a different mode — the **Genre Lift** applied to the same material, targeting the **field report** form:

---

*October 26, 2023. 11:30 AM. Reading Room, National Archives II, College Park, MD.*

*I requested Box 8 of the President's Commission staff files. The box contains the working notes of Norman Redlich, the assistant counsel who drafted the Warren Report's conclusions.*

*Redlich's handwriting is small and uniform. He wrote in black ink. The margins contain crossed-out sentences. One reads: "The evidence that there was a second gunman is not conclusive, but it is also not excludable." This sentence was removed from the final draft. Below it, in a different hand (possibly Rankin's), in pencil: "We cannot say this."*

*The sentence was replaced by: "The evidence does not establish that there was a second gunman." The substitution turns a claim about evidence into a claim about establishment. The two sentences have the same truth conditions — both are compatible with the existence of a second gunman — but opposite rhetorical force. The first invites investigation. The second closes it.*

*This is not a conspiracy. It is a structural edit. A document that says "the evidence is not excludable" is structurally different from a document that says "the evidence does not establish." The question of which one is true is a question about the prose, not about Dealey Plaza.*

*The structural type of the Warren Report is:*

$$\langle \text{𐑼} \cdot \text{𐑰} \cdot \text{𐑩} \cdot \text{𐑬} \cdot \text{𐑱} \cdot \text{𐑪} \cdot \text{𐑔} \cdot \text{𐑠} \cdot \text{𐑢} \cdot \text{𐑖} \cdot \text{𐑕} \cdot \text{𐑭} \rangle$$

*The topology is containment (𐑰: the Report contains its evidence, arranges it, encloses it). The criticality is sub-critical (𐑢: no phase transition). The stoichiometry is mass-produced (𐑕: many identical copies, each saying the same thing). The winding is integer (𐑭: the argument returns to its conclusion and can be traced forward and backward without contradiction).*

*The Report and the event are different structural types. They are not isomorphic. The question "was Oswald the lone gunman?" is a question about the event. The question "could the Report have said something different?" is a question about the prose. They are the same question stated in different registers. The grammar holds both.*

---

This is not better than the previous lift. It is differently constructed. The voice is not "community participant with a position" — it is "researcher who was in the archives and saw something." The authority comes from the field report form, not from the prose's internal structure. The reader trusts it because it has a date, a location, and a physical object.

The field report genre forced: H₂ (I was there), G_aleph (the crossed-out sentence, the different hand, the pencil), Γ_seq (the observation precedes the interpretation), R_lr (I am reporting to colleagues who know the basics — I do not explain who Redlich is or why the National Archives exists). None of these were achieved by consulting a primitive table. They were achieved by writing in the form.

The form is the lift. The lift is invisible because the form is conventional. A reader unfamiliar with the Imscribing Grammar does not see "the H primitive was promoted" — they see "someone went to the archives and found something."

That is the difference. The existing lifts make prose better. These seven lifts make prose **differently** — in ways that resist reverse-engineering because the controlling variable was never the primitive list.
