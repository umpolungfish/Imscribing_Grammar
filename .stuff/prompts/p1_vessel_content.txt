The grammar provides two things simultaneously: the vessel (form the crystal coordinate that
constitutes what a system is capable of being) and the content (fill the primitive algebra
operations that determine what that vessel holds). These are not separable. Formalize this.

Three theorems, in order of dependency:

  theorem form_uniqueness (M : ImscribableSystem) :
      ExistsUnique (fun c : Coordinate => Imscribes M c)

  theorem content_containment (M : ImscribableSystem) (c : Coordinate)
      (h : Imscribes M c) (r : Result) (hr : Reachable M r) :
      WithinAlgebra c r

  theorem vessel_fills_itself (M : ImscribableSystem) (c : Coordinate)
      (h : Imscribes M c) (r : Result) :
      Reachable M r <-> WithinAlgebra c r

vessel_fills_itself is the strong form and the non-trivial claim. form_uniqueness and
content_containment are its prerequisite lemmas.

Define the abstract types: ImscribableSystem, Coordinate, Result and the relations:
Imscribes, Reachable, WithinAlgebra as axioms since they are not yet in the library.
The theorems state the shape of what must be true; concrete instances are future work.

For each sorry: name the exact lemma that would discharge it.
The docstring for vessel_fills_itself must explain why a pure classifier
(form_uniqueness alone) does not give you content_containment.

Import from Imscribing.Primitives.Core and Imscribing.Primitives.Imscription where possible.
Add to lakefile.toml Imscribing globs. Run: cd ~/MillenniumAnkh && lake build Imscribing.VesselContent
