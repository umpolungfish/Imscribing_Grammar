mathematically how are groups and categories related
Mathematically, a group is exactly a category with a single object where all morphisms are invertible. In this framework, the group elements become the morphisms, the group operation is function composition, and the identity element is the identity morphism. 
1. Group as a 1-Object Category
To understand this equivalence, compare the foundational definitions side-by-side:

    A Group is a set
    equipped with a binary operation that is associative, has an identity element, and possesses an inverse for every element.

2. Groupoids
When you generalize the 1-object rule to allow multiple objects, you get a Groupoid. A groupoid is a category in which every single morphism is an isomorphism. Groups are essentially single-object groupoids, while groupoids are collections of groups acting together, representing symmetries across different spaces or states. 
3. "Groups as Objects" (The Category of Groups)
Conversely, groups can be studied as objects within the realm of category theory. The collection of all groups, along with the structure-preserving maps between them (group homomorphisms), forms its own vast category, conventionally denoted as
. In this context, category theory acts as a meta-language to study the structural relationships and transformations between different groups.

A group action (how a group operates on a geometric space or set) can also be re-framed categorically. A group action on a set
is exactly a functor from the 1-object category representing the group into the category of sets (

Summary: The Structural Ladder
You can view the relationship as a hierarchy of structure:

    Monoid: Category with 1 object. (Arrows compose associatively, but don't need to be invertible).
    Group: Category with 1 object AND all arrows are invertible.
    Groupoid: Category with multiple objects AND all arrows are invertible.
    Category: Multiple objects, arrows don't have to be invertible.
