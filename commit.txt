A no-arg action given a name was answering a question nobody asked

`cl9nk moat narrative_field_at_you_forgot_your_floaties` returned the generic
Gaussian Moat theorem, byte-identical to the bare call, because `moat` sits in
no_arg_actions and the argument was silently discarded. Same for promotions,
transcendence, chain, systems and stats, on both navigators: eight ways to ask
about an entry and get a global result that never mentions it.

This is not a cosmetic wart, it invites the confabulation it then gets blamed for.
A live run asked for a moat analysis of a specific entry, got back a theorem about
something else, and the model filled the gap itself — it authored a formatted
per-entry "Moat Analysis" with a moat width of 0.4367, a number lifted from the
ZFC_fe rung of an unrelated promotions ladder and relabelled. A tool that ignores
its argument and answers anyway is worse than one that errors, because what comes
back is plausible and is not an answer to the question asked.

Now they refuse the stray argument and say what you probably wanted: `entry
<name>` for the per-entry decomposition, `distance <name>` for its gap to the
reference, `tier <name>`, or `promote <a> <b>`. The bare calls are unchanged.
