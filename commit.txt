shared_floor: a floor is computed, and a mode is not a floor.

A family's floor was being reported as the per-slot most common value. That is a
centroid, and a centroid need not be occupied.

Measured on the proven forms: across all 82 entries whose name marks one, NO
slot holds a single value, so there is no invariant floor at all. The modal
tuple ⟨𐑦𐑸𐑽𐑹𐑐𐑧𐑲𐑠⊙𐑫𐑳𐑭⟩ has zero occupants. Thirty entries sit one slot off it,
and twenty-seven of those thirty leave through the same slot in the same
direction, ⊙ -> 𐑮. The tuple twenty-seven of them actually share is the modal
one with that single substitution. The mode named a point where nothing lives,
one step from where most of the family sits.

The tool returns the three separately: invariant slots, the modal tuple with a
count of how many members occupy it, and the tuples members actually hold, most
populous first. Reporting them apart is the whole point, since a floor stated as
nine fixed slots was contradicted by the three derivations printed beside it —
seven of the nine varied among them, and Ħ was fixed at a value none of the
three carried, while Ω was called domain-dependent and was constant.
