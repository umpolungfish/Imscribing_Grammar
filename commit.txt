Transitions are counted on the ring, and a statistic can be asked whether it
survives rotation.

A word is a cycle and ROTAT is the cyclic shift, so a word of length n has n
transitions rather than n-1. The one a linear read drops is the wrap from the
last opcode to the first, and in IMASM that is overwhelmingly TANCH -> VINIT,
the anchor returning to the source. Across k programs a linear read loses
exactly k such edges, all of them closing edges, and a table built without them
can show a rule as universal that the closing edges break. That is what a
130-transition count over 142 opcodes in twelve programs was: 142 minus twelve.

rotation_invariant takes any statistic over a word and runs it around the orbit.
Anything read from absolute position measures the cut rather than the word:
matrix rows, tetraktys tiers, odd against even positions. One rotation moves
every value into a different row. The total ordinal of a word survives; the sum
over even positions takes a different value at nearly every cut.

Both reachable as the imasm_transitions grammar tool, which reports the ring
count, the linear count, the closing edge, and whether each of the two
statistics most often read off a word is invariant. The kernel carries the same
as `trans`.
