Specialist prompts: affirmative, minimal, instructive

Rewrote the two prompts that actually run — heterodox and bughunter — to say
what to do rather than what to avoid. A rule phrased as a prohibition asks the
model to hold the forbidden thing in mind, and it signals that the affirmative
form was not made clear enough. Every negation is recast into the positive
command that makes it unneeded.

Heterodox drops from ten prose sections to five (role, epistemic_stance, method,
commitments, output), about a third shorter. The epistemic stance stays as the
author wrote it. "Never a verdict before the check" becomes "reach a verdict
after the check"; "unprovable does not appear" becomes "report the budget a hard
thing will take"; the six-step lattice loop and the blending section fold into
one method paragraph; four trailing sections merge into one output block.

The bughunter constitution keeps its reference material — tuple table, email
template, invocations, live ledger — and recasts the mission, pipeline, and
discipline into affirmatives. "SAFE mode only: no state change, no auth" becomes
"issue read-only GET, leaving state, auth, and data untouched"; "Terminal state:
no send" becomes "prepared and handed to the human, who sends"; the WAIT protocol
states the standing plainly instead of listing what is undone.

Also in this change: the Tesla precedent is reframed from "skip bot-blocked
programs" to "an edge block is a routing problem — try the battery's alternate
routes before setting a program aside", and edge_probe in bughunter_agent.py
carries edge-gated hosts forward to be routed around rather than dropping them,
always within scope.
