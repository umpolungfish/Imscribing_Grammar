The primitives take the IMASM alphabet, and Criticality keeps ⊙

    ⊢ Dimensionality  ⊣ Topology     > Relational     < Polarity
    ⋈ Fidelity        ⊤ Kinetics     ∈ Granularity    ∋ Grammar
    ⊙ Criticality     ⊥ Chirality    ⊞ Stoichiometry  ◻ Protection

The old set arrived already meaning other things. Γ is the gamma function, Σ a
summation, Φ a potential, Ω a domain, and a reader brings all of that to a slot
that only wants a position. The opcodes mean nothing on their own, which is their
whole qualification, and there were never two alphabets to map between: the
twelve axes and the twelve opcodes are the same twelve.

⊙ does not move. Two tables already put it with Criticality — cr3echrz's
opcodes.py has EVALT as ("⊙", "Criticality — evaluate-true gate") and ask_native's
learn.rs has the same pairing — and classic.rs says outright that IMSCRIB's ⊙
appearing as Criticality is not a collision but the same structure surfacing.
Pairing the two lists by slot position instead would have put ⊙ on Kinetics; the
anchor wins and Kinetics takes ⊤. That decision is what made the rest tractable:
eleven keys move rather than twelve, and no VALUE moves at all. The monad is a
legal value of Criticality and 5,122 of 8,032 entries carry "⊙": "⊙", key and
value the same character on one line. None of that had to be touched.

The migration went through parsed JSON, never text. Ð and Þ and Γ also open value
names like Ð_point in a different namespace that merely shares the character, and
every substitution here is anchored on a complete quoted token for the same
reason. 88,253 keys renamed in the canonical catalog and 87,846 in the live store
at ~/.imscrbgrmr, both backed up beside themselves, and every entry's twelve
values verified identical before and after.

Two things were restated where they should have been derived, and a rename is
exactly what finds those. space_search/primitives.py carried its own copy of
ORDINALS, WEIGHTS and PRIMITIVE_ORDER, so the two would have had to move in the
same commit forever and the one that was missed would go on answering; it imports
them now. registry.py's provenance hashed a hand-written slot list, and
slot_category_violations is keyed on the very names it validates, so a rename
would have made it fail OPEN: every .get() missing, every entry skipped, silence
instead of a report. It reads PRIMITIVE_ORDER now. The violations baseline
regenerated to the same 30 entries it held before, which is the proof it is still
looking.

Imscription.from_dict is the seam. It already accepted glyph keys or short ASCII
or long field names, and it takes the retired glyphs as a further fallback, so a
catalog written before this still loads in one place rather than twelve. to_dict
writes the new keys. Old and new keyed entries load to the identical tuple.

Verified rather than assumed: the twelve-axis round trip through
`imasm cycle tuple=⟨…⟩` recovers 12 of 12 axes exactly with the monad intact;
`imscribe join loki_laufeyjarson odinn_allfather` still meets at 𐑮 and joins at
𐑻; sync_catalog.sh reports all 8 consumers matching; gen_catalog_subset.py
regenerates 8,016 entries, one MORE than the committed snapshot and none fewer;
and mOMonadOS boots reporting ordinal faithfulness against the Lean canonical.
The ten entries it skips carry a legacy pre-Shavian `tuple` string and no glyph
keys at all; they were skipped before this too.

scripts/IG_catalog.json had drifted to 8,033 entries with one extra,
cat_translator, whose > slot holds a ◻ value and whose ⊤ slot holds an ∈ value.
It is preserved beside itself as .pre_rename_drift rather than merged, since
merging it would put a malformed tuple in the canonical catalog.
