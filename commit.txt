Record catalog provenance in a sidecar

The catalog stores name, description and twelve glyphs and nothing else, so an
assignment cannot be asked whether it was computed or asserted. That gap is
load-bearing: an entry at the top of the winding ladder is indistinguishable
from one that merely claims to be, and an entry whose description asserts its
own promotion is indistinguishable from one a verb walked there.

It demonstrated itself while this was being written. A well-formed entry,
g_lattice_investigation, appeared in the canonical catalog and nothing anywhere
records who wrote it or when — only the file mtime, which the next write
destroys.

The tuple fields are left alone, because eight consumers read that file and new
keys risk all of them. The record goes beside the catalog instead: first_seen,
last_changed, a hash of the twelve glyphs, and a change count. An entry whose
tuple never moves after it appears was asserted whole at registration; one that
moves was walked, and the count says by how many steps.

Not retroactive. Existing entries are stamped as of this write, which dates the
stamp and not their origin.
