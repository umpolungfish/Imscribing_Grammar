LOAD_IN_8BIT is real, and a CPU fallback announces itself

Two measured findings, after reading the model card and the canonical parser
rather than guessing again.

LOAD_IN_8BIT was implemented nowhere. Only the 4-bit branch existed, so exporting
it changed nothing: the same probe OOM'd byte-for-byte with the variable set and
unset. It builds a BitsAndBytesConfig now.

With 8-bit genuinely applied, the 27B still does not fit: 27 billion parameters at
one byte each is ~27 GB against 24 GB of card, accelerate tries to dispatch the
remainder to CPU or disk, bitsandbytes refuses without an explicit offload flag,
and the loader quietly retried on CPU. That is the whole explanation of the
⊙perator's empty turns — a 27B on CPU cannot finish a winding in any time the
loop waits. Not the parser, not the chat template, not the identity framing.

So the fallback is loud now: a banner on stderr naming the model, its size on
disk, the reason it did not fit, and what to do instead. A silent fallback turns
a hardware limit into an inscrutable empty answer, which is what cost most of
today.
